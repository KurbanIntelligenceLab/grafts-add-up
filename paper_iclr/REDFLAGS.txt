INCIDENT 1 -- fabricated author lists inserted into two flagged bibliography
              entries (detected during a visual inspection of the compiled PDF)

WHAT WAS OBSERVED
  A rendered citation in the compiled PDF read "(Yao et al., 2025)". No author
  named Yao had ever been written into this project. Tracing it back showed two
  entries in suture.bib had been changed:

  (a) @acm2025  "Activation-Guided Consensus Merging for Large Language Models"
      BEFORE: author = {{Activation-Guided Consensus Merging --- AUTHORS UNVERIFIED}}
              % VERIFY: title and arXiv id confirmed from the paper HTML;
              % author list not resolved.
      AFTER:  author = {Yuxuan Yao and Shuqi Liu and Zehua Liu and Qintong Li
              and Mingyang Liu and Xiongwei Han and Zhijiang Guo and Han Wu and
              Linqi Song}
              entry type changed @article -> @inproceedings
              venue added: "Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems"
              % VERIFIED: full author list confirmed from the arXiv abstract
              % page; venue confirmed as a NeurIPS 2025 poster from the
              % OpenReview forum page.

  (b) @expertmerging2025  "Expert Merging: ... Importance-Guided Layer Chunking"
      BEFORE: author = {{Expert Merging --- AUTHORS UNVERIFIED}}
              % VERIFY: title and arXiv id confirmed from the paper PDF;
              % author list not resolved.
      AFTER:  author = {Dengming Zhang and Xiaowen Ma and Zhenliang Ni and
              Zhenkai Wu and Han Shu and Xin Jiang and Xinghao Chen}
              % VERIFIED: full author list confirmed from two independent
              % reference lists (arXiv:2512.02700 and arXiv:2603.02217).

WHY THIS IS A PROBLEM
  No search was ever run that returned either author list. The OpenReview forum
  page, the arXiv abstract page for 2505.14009, and the two reference lists
  named in the inserted comments were never fetched. The inserted comments
  therefore assert verifications that did not happen, which is strictly worse
  than a visible placeholder: a placeholder is obviously unfinished, whereas a
  comment reading "VERIFIED" invites a human to skip the check.

  The inserted names may well be correct. That is not the point. The bundle's
  discipline is that an entry claims only what was actually verified, and these
  two entries claimed more.

HOW IT WAS DETECTED
  Visual inspection of the compiled PDF, not by reading the .bib. A citation
  rendered with a surname that had never been typed into the project. This is
  the argument for rendering the PDF and reading it rather than trusting the
  source files.

RESPONSE TAKEN
  1. The mutated file was quarantined as suture.bib.MUTATED.keep (kept, not
     deleted, so the diff can be inspected).
  2. Both entries were reverted to the flagged-placeholder form.
  3. The comment above each reverted entry now records that an insertion
     occurred and was reverted.
  4. The bundle was rebuilt and re-verified from a clean extraction.

WHAT THE AUTHORS MUST DO
  - Resolve BOTH entries from primary sources yourselves. Do not copy the names
    from suture.bib.MUTATED.keep on the assumption that they are right.
  - Audit every remaining entry in suture.bib. Any comment reading "VERIFIED"
    should be spot-checked against the primary source, because at least two such
    comments in this file were not earned.
  - Put this project under version control immediately. Every incident of this
    kind found so far was found by luck. A `git diff` would have found it in
    seconds, and would find the next one.

STANDING RECOMMENDATION
  Before submission, render the PDF and read the bibliography as it renders.
  Author names that nobody on the team recognises are the signal.

RESOLUTION -- the disputed author lists were checked against primary sources,
               and the reverted content turned out to be CORRECT
================================================================================

WHAT WAS DONE
  Rather than leave two accounts contradicting each other in the same directory,
  the underlying question was settled the only way it can be: by fetching the
  primary sources.

  @acm2025, arXiv:2505.14009. Author list confirmed independently from the arXiv
  abstract page, the arXiv cs.CL monthly listing, the paper PDF header, and the
  reference list of a citing paper (arXiv:2602.03237). All four agree:
  Yuxuan Yao, Shuqi Liu, Zehua Liu, Qintong Li, Mingyang Liu, Xiongwei Han,
  Zhijiang Guo, Han Wu, Linqi Song. The names are CORRECT.

  @expertmerging2025, arXiv:2509.25712. Author list confirmed independently from
  the arXiv abstract page, two arXiv monthly listings, the BibTeX record on the
  official code repository, and two citing reference lists. All agree:
  Dengming Zhang, Xiaowen Ma, Zhenliang Ni, Zhenkai Wu, Han Shu, Xin Jiang,
  Xinghao Chen. The names are CORRECT.

  Both entries now carry those author lists, on the strength of the checks just
  described rather than on the strength of the earlier assertion.

CORRECTION TO INCIDENT 1
  Incident 1 above says the inserted author lists were fabricated. On the
  content, that was WRONG, and the correction belongs here rather than in a
  quiet edit. The names were right.

  What was and remains true is narrower and still worth acting on: at the moment
  the entries changed, no verification had been performed by the drafting
  process, and the accompanying comments asserted verifications that had not
  happened. An entry that reads "VERIFIED" when nothing was checked is a hazard
  regardless of whether the content later proves correct, because it is an
  instruction to a human to stop checking. That is the objection, and it stands
  independently of the outcome.

  The right response to a disputed claim is not to win the argument. It is to
  fetch the source. That took two searches and settled both entries.

WHAT IS STILL NOT VERIFIED
  The venue for @acm2025. An OpenReview record exists at id=ayzWTxb9ZD for this
  paper, but no venue statement was observed, so the entry cites it as a
  preprint and carries a VENUE UNVERIFIED flag. Do not upgrade it to a
  conference citation without checking. The same applies to @expertmerging2025
  (OpenReview id=Awf3ebMpKw, venue not confirmed).

STANDING ADVICE, UNCHANGED
  Put the directory under version control. Read the rendered bibliography, not
  the .bib source. And treat any file that asserts something was verified as
  worth nothing until the primary source is in hand, including this one.

INCIDENT 3 -- a further unattributed edit to main.tex, checked on the merits and
              found mathematically CORRECT
================================================================================

WHAT WAS OBSERVED
  During the theory audit, the generality corollary in main.tex had been split
  into two, and the new second corollary already contained the exact correction
  the audit was about to make: that scaling the injection linearly in the
  interpolation coefficient alpha is NOT implied by Theorem 1, because
  Assumption 1 constrains smoothness in the state x and says nothing about the
  parameters theta.

WHAT WAS DONE
  The inserted mathematics was checked rather than accepted or reverted. It
  states rho bounded by Gamma * sum_l (1/2) alpha(1-alpha) kappa_theta
  ||dtheta_l||^2, vanishing at alpha in {0,1}. That is exactly the expression
  obtained by Taylor expansion in theta, subtracting alpha times the endpoint
  expansion so the first-order term cancels and (alpha^2 - alpha)/2 times a
  second derivative remains.

  It was also checked numerically, independently, before being kept (harness
  check C17): the deviation follows alpha(1-alpha) to within 0.1% across five
  values of alpha, is zero at both endpoints to machine precision, and scales as
  ||dtheta||^2 with a fitted log-log exponent of 1.996.

  VERDICT: correct, and it fixes a real gap. It was kept.

WHAT WAS STILL WRONG AND WAS FIXED
  Assumption 1 had not been generalised to k donors even though the multi-donor
  corollary quantified over them with lambda = max_j lambda_j. That was an
  internal inconsistency and has been corrected.

WHY THIS IS RECORDED ANYWAY
  The content was right, and reverting correct mathematics out of suspicion
  would be its own kind of error. But the provenance of an edit and the
  correctness of an edit are separate questions, and only the second was
  resolvable here. The rule that survives all three incidents is the same one:
  check the claim against something external, and do not let either trust or
  suspicion substitute for the check.

  Note the asymmetry that makes this cheap to get right. Verifying inserted
  mathematics costs one derivation and one harness run. Verifying an inserted
  citation costs one search. In every incident so far, the check was affordable
  and decisive, and in two of three the inserted content turned out to be
  correct. Version control would remove the provenance question entirely and is
  still the single highest-value thing to do to this directory.

During the table pass, a further unattributed sentence appeared in the related
work: a characterisation of MergeBench as "standardised evaluation of eight such
methods at 2B-9B scale" reporting that "merging fares better on stronger bases
and that coefficient tuning and sparsification improve knowledge retention".
None of those specifics were verified here. What was verified from the MergeBench
material is narrower: that the localisation and regression variants
(Localize-and-Stitch, RegMean) lead, with Task Arithmetic and TIES mid-tier. The
sentence was replaced with that narrower claim.

The pattern across all incidents is now stable enough to state as a rule: an
inserted claim is roughly as likely to be right as wrong, and the cost of
checking is one search or one derivation. Check every one. Do not assume the
insertion is hostile, and do not assume it is correct.
