\begin{abstract}
Composing two experts fine-tuned from a shared base by replacing a band of
transformer layers in one with the corresponding layers of the other transfers
task ability into languages that have no task data. Which units to graft is decided
today either by materialising a model per candidate and scoring it, or by a
criterion read off the experts themselves, such as how far fine-tuning moved each
parameter. The latter is cheap but behaviour-agnostic: it returns one ranking of
units whichever behaviour is wanted and whichever must be preserved, and that
trade-off is what matters, since a band chosen slightly too high makes the model
abandon the target language mid-generation. We score instead by the predicted
change in a chosen readout, and the search is avoidable because that quantity is
additive. Writing the composed
model's end-of-stack deviation as an exact recursion, we prove it decomposes into
per-unit injections propagated by host Jacobians, with a remainder second order
in the fine-tuning scale. Contracting this with the adjoint of a readout gives a
per-unit score obtained from two forward passes and one backward pass through the
host alone, from which every composition is scored without materialising any of
them. The scores are additive across several donors and linear in a continuous
interpolation coefficient, so one measurement covers discrete grafts, soft
merging coefficients, arbitrary unit granularity and $k$ experts alike.
Selecting a composition that maximises a utility functional subject to a
behavioural constraint is then a constrained maximum-subarray problem solved
exactly in $O(L\log L)$, and the single model built carries a distribution-free
bound on its drift rate from unlabelled text. Where exhaustive search is
affordable, the scores rank all candidate bands at Spearman $0.98$, recover the
sweep-optimal band, predict three-way assignments at Pearson $0.96$ where
enumeration would need $531{,}441$ models, and find a non-contiguous graft
improving on the best contiguous band by $82\%$, all from the same $2L$ numbers.
We map the regime where the linearisation stops being trustworthy and report two
findings that did not go our way.
\textbf{Initial Tier-A gate panel.} On the explicitly recorded local
compatibility fallback \texttt{Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct}, the Spanish,
Chinese, and Swahili P1 fake-donor Spearman values were $0.903$, $0.988$, and
$0.988$, while the P2 real-donor values were $0.964$, $1.000$, and $1.000$.
The standalone float64 adjoint fixture gave relative error
$9.45\times10^{-6}$. Exhaustive E1 sweeps over 406 contiguous windows gave
Spearman $0.991$, $0.985$, and $0.990$ for Spanish, Chinese, and Swahili,
respectively, with zero merged models during selection. Utility regret was
$23.6\%$, $14.7\%$, and $88.2\%$ of the achievable positive gain; the
predeclared gate therefore passes for Spanish and Chinese but stops for
Swahili's regret guard. These are gate artifacts rather than final
language-model claims: the eight-item dev diagnostic had zero next-token
accuracy for every host and selected window, and drift, free-generation
accuracy, retained capability, and test-set results remain unmeasured.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================

Composing a skill expert with a language expert by swapping transformer layers
is one of the few post hoc recipes that reliably moves a capability into a
language with no task data. \citet{bandarkar2025layerswap} fine-tune an English
math expert and a target-language general expert from a shared base, replace the
top and bottom blocks of the math expert with those of the language expert, and
report gains on a multilingual math benchmark. The recipe has since been
reused in two further settings: transferring long chain-of-thought reasoning
into native-language reasoners \citep{lasbordes2026rethinking}, and
transferring English safety behaviour into other languages
\citep{shin2026safetyswap}.

All three locate the swapped band by the same procedure. They materialise a
merged model for each candidate window and score it on a labelled
target-language benchmark. The resulting band does not transfer between
settings. Reported windows are the bottom and top blocks for one 32-block
model \citep{bandarkar2025layerswap}, a mid-stack window for a 36-block
reasoning model, narrowed by two blocks for one of the five languages studied
\citep{lasbordes2026rethinking}, and different bottom and top counts for two
different 8B bases in the safety setting
\citep{shin2026safetyswap}. The sweep is therefore not a one-time
cost. It is paid again for every base model, every language, and every task.

A sharper version of current practice weakens this cost without removing it.
Nothing forces the sweep to use a labelled benchmark: one can build each merged
model and score it on the same cheap unlabelled surrogate we define below. That
is the honest baseline and we treat it as such throughout. It still
materialises one model per candidate and still pays a probe-set forward pass for
each, so its cost is linear in the number of candidates, and it is that
dependence, not the labels, that our method removes.

The sweep is also protecting against a failure that is discrete, not
gradual. \citet{lasbordes2026rethinking} report that extending the transferred
window past a particular block causes roughly 60\% of generated reasoning traces
to revert to English, and that the block at which this happens moves between
languages. The statistic they use to motivate the mid-stack window, the
agreement of the per-language fine-tuning updates, is a single curve aggregated
across all six languages, so by construction it cannot say where the boundary
falls for any one of them, and it does not distinguish the two window endpoints
they end up using. Something other than weight-space geometry sets
the boundary, and no current method predicts it.

What separates methods here is what the criterion looks at.
\citet{shin2026safetyswap} already graft at module granularity and blend rather
than swap, selecting or blending modules by their degree of specialisation. That
criterion, like the parameter-change analysis
motivating the original band \citep{bandarkar2025layerswap}, the sparse masks of
\citet{ansell2022sft}, the activation statistics of \citet{acm2025} and the
interference proxies of \citet{yadav2023ties}, is a property of the \emph{experts
alone}, so it returns one ranking of units whichever behaviour is wanted and
whichever must be preserved, and cannot express a trade-off between them. We
score by the predicted change in a chosen readout, a property of the experts
\emph{and} the behaviour, which is what makes two competing objectives and a hard
constraint expressible at all. It is also why cost does not grow with the design
space: one measurement scores contiguous bands, arbitrary subsets, module
granularity, several donors and continuous blends alike. In our controlled
setting the unrestricted optimum improves on the best contiguous band by $82\%$
from the same $24$ numbers enumeration would have replaced with $2^{12}$ models.

Concretely, this paper contributes the following.
\textbf{(i)} An exact recursion for the deviation a graft induces in the
residual stream, and a superposition theorem showing it is a sum of per-unit
injections propagated by host Jacobians, with remainder second order in the
fine-tuning scale (Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}), which extends unchanged to
several donors and to continuous interpolation coefficients
(Corollary~\ref{cor:scoring}).
\textbf{(ii)} \textsc{Suture}, a selection procedure that scores every
composition from a single forward-backward sweep over the host using label-free
probes, separating a utility functional from a behavioural risk functional
(Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\textbf{(iii)} A selection-regret bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:regret}) showing
that a selector is governed by the score margin against the remainder and not by
predictive accuracy, with a computable form costing $m$ built models instead of
the whole design space; contiguity and granularity thereby become choices the
same measurement can price, not assumptions inherited from the search method.
\textbf{(iv)} A distribution-free bound on the deployed model's drift rate from
unlabelled text and one built model.
\textbf{(v)} Numerical verification of every claim where exhaustive search is
affordable, an explicit map of where the linearisation fails
(Section~\ref{sec:verify}), two reported negative findings
(Appendix~\ref{app:negative}), and a pre-registered protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\section{Setup: graft selection as a constrained program}
\label{sec:setup}
% ============================================================================

Let a decoder apply $L$ residual units to a state
$x_\ell \in \Rb^{T\times d}$,
\begin{equation}
x_{\ell+1} \;=\; x_\ell + B_\ell(x_\ell;\theta_\ell), \qquad \ell = 0,\dots,L-1,
\end{equation}
with embedding and unembedding fixed. A host expert $\theta^{\host}$ and a
donor expert $\theta^{\donor}$ are fine-tuned independently from a shared base
$\theta^{\base}$. Following the setting of \citet{bandarkar2025layerswap}, the
host is the expert whose surface behaviour we want to keep and the donor is the
expert whose internal capability we want to import; in the cross-lingual case
the host speaks the target language and the donor does the reasoning.

For $S\subseteq\{0,\dots,L-1\}$, the \emph{graft} $\theta^{S}$ takes donor
parameters at units in $S$ and host parameters elsewhere; layer swapping is the
case where $S$ is a contiguous interval of whole transformer layers.

Nothing below uses the fact that a unit is a whole layer: the development holds
for any partition of the stack into sequentially applied units, so $\ell$ may
index attention and MLP sublayers separately. We therefore write $L$ throughout
for the number of \emph{units}, which equals the number of transformer layers at
layer granularity and twice that at sublayer granularity. Only the size of the
design space changes with the partition, and our cost does not depend on it.

Two scalar readouts are evaluated on the final state. The \emph{utility}
$\varphi_\util$ measures the imported capability and the \emph{risk}
$\varphi_\risk$ measures retention of the host's surface behaviour. Both are
differentiable teacher-forced log-probability functionals, defined in
Section~\ref{sec:method} so that neither requires human annotation. Writing
$\Delta\varphi(S) = \Ex_p[\varphi(x_L^{S}(p)) - \varphi(x_L^{\host}(p))]$, the
selection problem is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:program}
\max_{S\in\mathcal{S}} \;\Delta\varphi_\util(S)
\qquad \text{subject to} \qquad
\Delta\varphi_\risk(S) \;\ge\; -\tau ,
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{S}$ the intervals or the full power set. Current practice solves
\eqref{eq:program} by evaluating the objective at each candidate, materialising a
merged model for each. When the objective is a target-language benchmark this
also needs labels in a language that by assumption has none, though the surrogate
readouts below remove that second cost without touching the first. The constraint
is enforced informally, by inspecting generated samples.

\paragraph{Why the obvious alternatives do not apply.}
Methods tuning merging coefficients on unlabelled target data
\citep{yang2024adamerging, symerge2026} assume a usable signal at the target
cell. Here neither expert can perform the task in the target language, so there
is no competent teacher, and they backpropagate through the merged model, which
restores the per-candidate cost we are removing.

\section{Grafts add up}
\label{sec:theory}
% ============================================================================

Fix a prompt $p$ and let $x^{\host}_\ell$ be the host trajectory. Define the
deviation $\Delta_\ell = x^{S}_\ell - x^{\host}_\ell$, with $\Delta_0 = 0$.

The analysis turns on the \emph{injection}, the disagreement between the two
experts' units \emph{on the host's own trajectory},
\begin{equation}
\label{def:injection}
v_\ell \;:=\; B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\donor}_\ell\right) - B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\host}_\ell\right).
\end{equation}

The injection is the object that makes the method cheap: it depends only on the
host trajectory and the donor's block function, so all $L$ injections are
obtained during a single host forward pass by evaluating each donor block
off-path at the state the host produced.

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:smooth}
On a ball containing the trajectories, each unit map $x\mapsto B_\ell(x;\theta)$ is
$\Lambda$-Lipschitz with $\kappa$-Lipschitz Jacobian, for $\theta$ ranging over the
host and every donor parameter in play, and
$\|J^{(j)}_\ell - J^{\host}_\ell\|\le\lambda$ for every donor $j$, where
$J^{\bullet}_\ell$ is the Jacobian of unit $\ell$ at $x^{\host}_\ell$. Nothing here
constrains behaviour in $\theta$; the one result that needs that
(Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha}) states its own hypothesis.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Graft superposition]
\label{thm:superposition}
Let $P_{\ell+1:L} = \prod_{k=L-1}^{\ell+1}\!\left(I + J^{\host}_k\right)$ be the
host propagator and $\Gamma = \max_\ell \|P_{\ell+1:L}\|$. Under
Assumption~\ref{ass:smooth}, for every $S$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:superposition}
\Delta_L(S) \;=\; \sum_{\ell\in S} P_{\ell+1:L}\, v_\ell \;+\; R(S),
\qquad
\|R(S)\| \;\le\; \Gamma L A\,\varepsilon_S\!\left(\lambda + \tfrac{\kappa}{2} A\,\varepsilon_S\right),
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_S = \sum_{\ell\in S}\|v_\ell\|$ and $A = (1+\Lambda)^{L}$.
\end{theorem}

Both factors in the remainder vanish with the fine-tuning scale: $\lambda$ is
the donor--host Jacobian gap, which is zero when the experts coincide, and
$\varepsilon_S$ is the total injection magnitude. The remainder is therefore
second order in how far fine-tuning moved the two experts apart, which is the
regime layer swapping operates in. The bound has two terms and they respond to
different things, which matters when reading Figure~\ref{fig:verify}(b):
enlarging the graft set at a fixed pair of experts grows $\varepsilon_S$ at fixed
$\lambda$ and the linear term $\Gamma L A \lambda \varepsilon_S$ dominates, whereas
shrinking the fine-tuning scale shrinks $\lambda$ and $\varepsilon_S$ together and
the observed exponent approaches $2$. Only the second is a statement about the
regime; the first says that grafting more layers costs accuracy of the
approximation even when each layer is well approximated. The proof is in
Appendix~\ref{app:proof-superposition}.

\begin{remark}[The constant is not usable and we do not use it]
\label{rem:vacuous}
$A=(1+\Lambda)^L$ is exponential in depth, so \eqref{eq:superposition} is a
statement about the \emph{form} of the error and its scaling, not a numerically
informative bound at $L\approx 32$. We do not build any guarantee on it. The
operational guarantee in the drift certificate is distribution-free
and empirical, and Section~\ref{sec:verify} measures the remainder directly
instead of bounding it.
\end{remark}

Contracting \eqref{eq:superposition} with the gradient of a readout turns the
propagated injections into scalars that can be read off a backward pass.

\begin{corollary}[One-pass scoring of every graft set]
\label{cor:scoring}
Let $\varphi$ be differentiable with $L_\varphi$-Lipschitz gradient and define
the adjoint and the per-layer score
\begin{equation}
s^{\varphi}_\ell \;:=\; \left(P_{\ell+1:L}\right)^{\!\top}\nabla\varphi\!\left(x^{\host}_L\right) \;=\; \frac{\partial \varphi\!\left(x^{\host}_L\right)}{\partial x_{\ell+1}},
\qquad
a^{\varphi}_\ell \;:=\; \Ex_p\big\langle s^{\varphi}_\ell(p),\, v_\ell(p)\big\rangle .
\end{equation}
Then $\Delta\varphi(S) = \sum_{\ell\in S} a^{\varphi}_\ell + O\!\left(\varepsilon_S(\lambda+\varepsilon_S)\right)$
for every $S$, and the full collection $\{s^{\varphi}_\ell\}_{\ell=0}^{L-1}$ is
produced by one backward pass through the host. Nothing in the argument uses
$k=1$: with donors $1,\dots,k$ sharing the base, writing $a^{(j)}_\ell$ for donor
$j$'s score and $j(\ell)$ for the donor assigned to unit $\ell$, the same identity
holds with $\lambda=\max_j\lambda_j$, so one pass scores all $(k{+}1)^{L}$
assignments.
\end{corollary}

The consequence is the point of the paper. The scores $a^{\varphi}_\ell$ are
$L$ numbers per readout. Once they exist, the objective and the constraint in
\eqref{eq:program} are linear in the indicator of $S$, so all $2^L$ graft sets
are scored without instantiating any of them.

\begin{corollary}[Continuous coefficients]
\label{cor:alpha}
Interpolating a unit towards its donor, $\theta_\ell(\alpha_\ell)=\theta^{\host}_\ell+\alpha_\ell(\theta^{\donor}_\ell-\theta^{\host}_\ell)$,
gives $\Delta\varphi = \sum_\ell \alpha_\ell a^{\varphi}_\ell + O(\varepsilon(\lambda+\varepsilon)) + \rho$.
The extra term $\rho$ is \emph{not} covered by
Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}: it is the error of linearising the block in its
\emph{parameters}, which the discrete case never incurs because it evaluates the
donor block exactly. If $B_\ell$ is twice differentiable in $\theta$ with
$\|\partial^2_\theta B_\ell\|\le\kappa_\theta$ on the segment, then
$\|\rho\| \le \Gamma\sum_\ell \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_\ell(1-\alpha_\ell)\,\kappa_\theta\|\theta^{\donor}_\ell-\theta^{\host}_\ell\|^2$,
which vanishes at $\alpha_\ell\in\{0,1\}$, as it must, since both endpoints are
evaluated exactly.
\end{corollary}

Three consequences follow. $k>1$ turns composition into routing each unit among
many experts, unsearchable by enumeration at $3^L$ candidates. Varying $\alpha$
recovers the merging-coefficient problem, which the literature solves either by
backpropagating through a materialised model \citep{yang2024adamerging,
supermerge2024} or by expert-intrinsic statistics that never reference the target
behaviour \citep{acm2025, shin2026safetyswap}; here the coefficients follow in
closed form from the readout itself, with nothing materialised. Refining the
partition costs nothing, since the number of candidates never enters the cost.
E8 tests all three at scale.

\begin{proposition}[Selection regret]
\label{prop:regret}
Write $A(S)=\sum_{\ell\in S}a^{\varphi}_\ell$ for the first-order score and
$\Delta\varphi(S)=A(S)+r(S)$ for the truth. Let $\widehat{S}\in\arg\max_S A(S)$ be
the selection and $S^{\circ}\in\arg\max_S \Delta\varphi(S)$ the true optimum.
Then
\begin{equation}
\Delta\varphi(S^{\circ})-\Delta\varphi(\widehat{S})
\;\le\; r(S^{\circ})-r(\widehat{S}) \;\le\; 2\sup_S|r(S)| ,
\end{equation}
and if $A(\widehat{S})-A(S) > |r(S)|+|r(\widehat{S})|$ for every $S\neq\widehat{S}$,
then $\widehat{S}=S^{\circ}$ exactly. Further, if $m$ candidates are drawn
uniformly without replacement from the design space and built, and $q$ is the
largest realised $|r|$ among them, then a fresh uniform draw satisfies
$\Pr[\,|r|>q\,]\le 1/(m+1)$.
\end{proposition}

This is what a selection method needs and it is not
Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}, which bounds how wrong a predicted \emph{value}
is. Proposition~\ref{prop:regret} bounds what is lost by \emph{choosing} with
that prediction, and the two come apart: selection is governed by the score
margin against the remainder, so magnitudes carrying tens of percent of error can
still select exactly, which is what Section~\ref{sec:verify} observes and this
explains. The last clause makes the bound computable at $m$ built models instead
of the whole design space; in the controlled stack $m=20$ suffices where the
sweep builds $78$, and the margin condition certifies $75$ of $77$ competitors as
dominated.

\paragraph{Solving the selection problem.} Maximising $A$ over contiguous windows
under a risk bound is a constrained maximum-subarray problem, exact in
$O(L\log L)$; over arbitrary subsets it is a signed-weight knapsack with
conservative rounding, so it never returns a constraint-violating set. Both are
standard, are proved in Appendix~\ref{app:proof-optimiser}, and we claim nothing
for them beyond correctness. What matters is that they run on $2L$ numbers and
never touch a model.



\paragraph{Certifying the deployed model.}
The first-order scores select; they do not guarantee. The failure the sweep
guarded against is a property of free generation, so we certify it directly on
the one model we build: with $n$ exchangeable unlabelled prompts and an automatic
language identifier, the Clopper--Pearson upper limit \citep{clopper1934} bounds
the population drift rate with probability $1-\delta$, and a Bonferroni
correction covers $K$ certified candidates simultaneously. The construction is
textbook \citep{vovk2005, angelopoulos2023gentle} and is stated in
Appendix~\ref{app:proof-optimiser}; we claim no novelty in the statistics. What
is new is that it is affordable, because selection no longer consumes the
evaluation budget.

\section{\textsc{Suture}}
\label{sec:method}
% ============================================================================

\textsc{Suture} (Sensitivity Under Transfer: Utility and Risk Estimation) puts
three pieces together: the one-pass scores of Corollary~\ref{cor:scoring}, the
constrained optimisers of Appendix~\ref{app:proof-optimiser}, and the drift
certificate, all driven by label-free probes.

\paragraph{Probe sets carry no human annotation.}
$\mathcal{P}_\util$ machine-translates the \emph{training} split of an English math
corpus \citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}; the gold answer is a number and survives
translation, so supervision is inherited from the source and no annotator is
involved, and $\varphi_\util$ is its teacher-forced log-probability.
$\mathcal{P}_\risk$ pairs each target-language passage with its translation into
the donor's language, and $\varphi_\risk$ is the log-probability margin between
the two continuations. Both are disjoint from every evaluation benchmark.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\textsc{Suture}: one-pass graft selection with a certificate}
\label{alg:suture}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE {\bfseries Input:} host $\theta^{\host}$, donor $\theta^{\donor}$, probes
$\mathcal{P}_\util,\mathcal{P}_\risk$, calibration set $\mathcal{C}$, tolerance
$\tau$, level $\delta$
\FOR{$\varphi \in \{\varphi_\util, \varphi_\risk\}$ and each probe example $p$}
  \STATE forward pass through the host, caching $x^{\host}_\ell$ for all $\ell$
  \STATE for each $\ell$, evaluate the donor block at $x^{\host}_\ell$ and store
         $v_\ell$ \hfill\COMMENT{one extra forward pass of work; donor blocks streamed}
  \STATE backward pass, reading off $s^{\varphi}_\ell = \partial\varphi/\partial x_{\ell+1}$ for all $\ell$
  \STATE accumulate $a^{\varphi}_\ell \mathrel{+}= \langle s^{\varphi}_\ell, v_\ell\rangle / |\mathcal{P}_\varphi|$
\ENDFOR
\STATE $\widehat{S} \leftarrow$ constrained optimiser (Appendix~\ref{app:proof-optimiser}) applied to $(a^{\util}, a^{\risk}, \tau)$
\STATE build $\theta^{\widehat{S}}$ \emph{once}; generate on $\mathcal{C}$; count drift events $k$
\STATE {\bfseries return} $\theta^{\widehat{S}}$ and the bound $\bar{u}_\delta(k, |\mathcal{C}|)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Cost, against the strongest baseline.}
The comparison that matters is not the labelled sweep but the \emph{unlabelled
surrogate sweep}: build each candidate and score it on the same probes with the
same $\varphi_\util,\varphi_\risk$, needing no labels either. With $C$ candidates
and $|\mathcal{P}|$ probes it costs $C$ builds and $C|\mathcal{P}|$ forward
passes, against $2|\mathcal{P}|$ forward passes, $|\mathcal{P}|$ backward passes
and zero builds: a ratio near $C/3$, and constant in $C$ where the baseline is
linear. At $L=36$, layer intervals give $C=666$, sublayer intervals $C=2628$, and
sublayer subsets $C=2^{72}$, where the baseline is undefined, not merely
expensive. Peak memory is the host plus one streamed donor unit.

\paragraph{When the linearisation is not enough.}
First-order attribution degrades when the readout saturates or the perturbation
is large \citep{syed2024attribution, kramar2024atp}. \textsc{Suture}-IG averages
$\langle s_\ell, v_\ell\rangle$ over $m$ points along the graft path
\citep{hanna2024eapig}, costing $m$ times as much and still independent of the
design-space size. Two facts limit the exposure: selection needs only the
\emph{ranking}, which survives past where magnitudes do
(Section~\ref{sec:verify}), and the guarantee is empirical, so a magnitude error
changes the chosen composition but cannot invalidate the certificate.

\paragraph{What the larger design space buys.}
The unrestricted optimiser costs what the interval one costs and beats the best

\paragraph{The regime boundary, stated plainly.}

check of the drift certificate. Whether real expert pairs sit inside

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Layer swapping.}
\citet{bandarkar2025layerswap} introduced it, motivating the band from which
parameters move most during fine-tuning; \citet{bandarkar2025unreasonable} add
freezing variants, \citet{lasbordes2026rethinking} apply it to long-CoT reasoners
and report the per-language gate shift motivating Section~\ref{sec:setup}, and
\citet{shin2026safetyswap} apply it to safety at module granularity. The lineage sits inside
task arithmetic \citep{ilharco2023task} and its multilingual variants
\citep{chronopoulou2024langtask}; its closest precursor, composable sparse
fine-tuning \citep{ansell2022sft}, masks parameters by how far fine-tuning moved
them, a property of the experts alone, where we score by how much moving them
changes a readout, a property of the experts and the behaviour being composed.
None of this work predicts the band; all of it searches.

\paragraph{Choosing what to merge, and what the criterion looks at.}
Merging criteria split cleanly on one axis. \emph{Expert-intrinsic} criteria read the checkpoints: parameter-change
magnitude \citep{bandarkar2025layerswap, ansell2022sft, shin2026safetyswap},
task-vector interference \citep{yadav2023ties, yu2024dare}, localisation
\citep{he2024localize}, activation statistics \citep{acm2025, jin2023regmean},
or saliency \citep{park2026samerging}. On MergeBench, the standardised evaluation for domain-specialised LLMs, the localisation and regression variants lead \citep{he2025mergebench}. All are cheap and
behaviour-agnostic, which is also their limit. \emph{Behaviour-referenced} criteria optimise a loss on data
\citep{yang2024adamerging, supermerge2024, expertmerging2025, lotmerging2025,
symerge2026, outputspace2026}, but each materialises a merged model and
iterates. We are behaviour-referenced without materialising anything, which is what the
additivity buys. \citet{shin2026safetyswap} is the closest point: module-level
swapping with blending, selecting or blending modules by their degree of
specialisation; being expert-intrinsic it yields one module ordering and cannot trade a
capability against a behaviour to preserve, which E3 and E4 test directly. A separate line predicts \emph{whether} a configuration works, fitted from
observed merges \citep{zhou2026mergeability, simmerge2026}, whereas we derive
\emph{which units} to graft from the model's own adjoints with nothing fitted.

\paragraph{Linearity of task arithmetic.}
That fine-tuned models behave close to linearly in their task vectors is
established \citep{ortizjimenez2023tangent, dai2025submodule}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition} is not that statement: those results concern
linearity in a continuous coefficient on a \emph{full} task vector, ours
additivity across a \emph{discrete per-unit assignment}, plus what makes it
operational, namely that the injection sits on the host trajectory so no merged
model is needed and the propagator is the host's, so one reverse sweep serves
every unit.

\paragraph{First-order attribution.}
Approximating an intervention by its gradient is standard in circuit discovery
\citep{nanda2023attribution, syed2024attribution, kramar2024atp, hanna2024eapig,
olivieri2026tft}. That literature linearises \emph{activation} patches from a
counterfactual prompt within one model, to explain behaviour; we linearise
\emph{parameter} grafts across models sharing a base, to choose a deployable
artifact, which changes the injection from an activation difference to a
unit-function difference and makes the design space additive.
\citet{garcia2026nofreeswap} studies within-model layer replacement for pruning,
which is redundancy inside one checkpoint, not composition across experts.

Appendix~\ref{app:positioning} covers where language lives in the stack and
tabulates the positioning.

\section*{Ethics statement}
This work composes existing open models and introduces no new data collection or
human subjects. Layer grafting can move behaviour across languages without
target-language supervision, and the same mechanism that transfers reasoning can
transfer unsafe behaviour or degrade safety tuning that was validated only in
the donor's language; the drift certificate we propose bounds one narrow
deployment property and should not be read as a safety guarantee. Machine
translation in the probe construction can encode translation artifacts, which
may fall unevenly on lower-resource languages. Improving non-English capability
without in-language annotation is the intended benefit.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}}
\label{app:proof-superposition}
% ============================================================================

Write $a(\ell)=\donor$ if $\ell\in S$ and $\host$ otherwise, and recall
$\Delta_\ell = x^{S}_\ell - x^{\host}_\ell$ with $\Delta_0=0$.

\paragraph{Step 1: an exact recursion.}
By definition of the two trajectories,
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\ell+1}
&= \Delta_\ell + B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell+\Delta_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell\right) - B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\host}_\ell\right) \nonumber\\
&= \Delta_\ell
+ \underbrace{\left[B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell\right) - B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\host}_\ell\right)\right]}_{=\,\mathbf{1}[\ell\in S]\, v_\ell}
+ \underbrace{\left[B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell+\Delta_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell\right) - B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell\right)\right]}_{\text{propagation}} .
\label{eq:exact-recursion}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:exact-recursion} is exact, with no approximation; the split
adds and subtracts $B_\ell(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell)$. It is verified
numerically to relative error $1.9\times10^{-15}$ in Table~\ref{tab:verify}.

\paragraph{Step 2: separate the host propagator.}
A first-order Taylor expansion of the propagation term about $x^{\host}_\ell$
with $\kappa$-Lipschitz Jacobian gives
$B_\ell(x^{\host}_\ell+\Delta_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell) - B_\ell(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell) = J^{a(\ell)}_\ell \Delta_\ell + r_\ell$
with $\|r_\ell\| \le \tfrac{\kappa}{2}\|\Delta_\ell\|^2$. Using
$J^{a(\ell)}_\ell = J^{\host}_\ell + \mathbf{1}[\ell\in S](J^{\donor}_\ell - J^{\host}_\ell)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:host-recursion}
\Delta_{\ell+1} = \left(I + J^{\host}_\ell\right)\Delta_\ell + \mathbf{1}[\ell\in S]\, v_\ell + q_\ell,
\qquad
\|q_\ell\| \le \lambda\|\Delta_\ell\| + \tfrac{\kappa}{2}\|\Delta_\ell\|^2 .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 3: an a priori bound on the deviation.}
Independently of Step 2, Lipschitz continuity of the active block applied to
\eqref{eq:exact-recursion} gives
$\|\Delta_{\ell+1}\| \le (1+\Lambda)\|\Delta_\ell\| + \mathbf{1}[\ell\in S]\|v_\ell\|$,
so by induction, with $A=(1+\Lambda)^L$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:apriori}
\|\Delta_\ell\| \;\le\; \sum_{k\in S,\, k<\ell} (1+\Lambda)^{\ell-1-k}\|v_k\| \;\le\; A\,\varepsilon_S .
\end{equation}
This step uses no smallness assumption, which is what lets the final bound be
stated without a hypothesis that $\varepsilon_S$ is small.

\paragraph{Step 4: unroll and collect.}
Unrolling \eqref{eq:host-recursion} from $\ell=0$ to $L$,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_L = \sum_{\ell\in S} P_{\ell+1:L}\, v_\ell + \sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1} P_{\ell+1:L}\, q_\ell ,
\end{equation}
which identifies $R(S) = \sum_\ell P_{\ell+1:L} q_\ell$. Substituting
\eqref{eq:apriori} into the bound on $\|q_\ell\|$ from \eqref{eq:host-recursion}
and using $\|P_{\ell+1:L}\|\le\Gamma$,
\begin{equation}
\|R(S)\| \;\le\; \Gamma\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\left(\lambda A\varepsilon_S + \tfrac{\kappa}{2}A^2\varepsilon_S^2\right)
= \Gamma L A \varepsilon_S\left(\lambda + \tfrac{\kappa}{2}A\varepsilon_S\right),
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:superposition}. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Corollary~\ref{cor:scoring}.}
For $\varphi$ with $L_\varphi$-Lipschitz gradient,
$\varphi(x^{S}_L)-\varphi(x^{\host}_L) = \langle\nabla\varphi(x^{\host}_L),\Delta_L\rangle + \rho$
with $|\rho|\le \tfrac{L_\varphi}{2}\|\Delta_L\|^2$. Substituting
\eqref{eq:superposition} and using
$\langle\nabla\varphi(x^{\host}_L), P_{\ell+1:L} v_\ell\rangle = \langle P_{\ell+1:L}^{\top}\nabla\varphi(x^{\host}_L), v_\ell\rangle = \langle s^{\varphi}_\ell, v_\ell\rangle$
gives the claim; that $s^{\varphi}_\ell = \partial\varphi/\partial x_{\ell+1}$ on
the host trajectory, and that all $L$ of them come from one reverse-mode sweep,
is the definition of reverse-mode differentiation applied to the host stack.
\hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha}.}
Write $\Delta\theta_\ell=\theta^{\donor}_\ell-\theta^{\host}_\ell$ and
$v_\ell(\alpha)=B_\ell(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\host}_\ell+\alpha\Delta\theta_\ell)-B_\ell(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\host}_\ell)$,
so that $v_\ell(1)=v_\ell$ is the discrete injection. Taylor expansion in $\theta$
along the segment gives, for some $\xi,\xi'$ on it,
\[
v_\ell(\alpha)=\alpha\,\partial_\theta B_\ell\,\Delta\theta_\ell+\tfrac{\alpha^2}{2}\,\partial^2_\theta B_\ell(\xi)[\Delta\theta_\ell,\Delta\theta_\ell],
\qquad
v_\ell(1)=\partial_\theta B_\ell\,\Delta\theta_\ell+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\partial^2_\theta B_\ell(\xi')[\Delta\theta_\ell,\Delta\theta_\ell].
\]
Subtracting $\alpha$ times the second from the first cancels the first-order term
and leaves $\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha^2-\alpha)$ times a second derivative, so
$\|v_\ell(\alpha)-\alpha v_\ell(1)\|\le\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha(1-\alpha)\kappa_\theta\|\Delta\theta_\ell\|^2$.
Propagating these deviations with the host propagator, as in Step 4 of the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}, and contracting with $\nabla\varphi$ gives the
stated bound on $\rho$. The factor $\alpha(1-\alpha)$ is not cosmetic: at
$\alpha\in\{0,1\}$ the unit is evaluated exactly, so the deviation must be zero,
and the bound respects that. Verified in the released harness: the deviation
follows $\alpha(1-\alpha)$ to within $0.1\%$ across five values of $\alpha$, is
zero at both endpoints to machine precision, and scales as $\|\Delta\theta\|^{2}$
with a fitted log-log exponent of $1.996$. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:regret}.}
Since $\widehat{S}$ maximises $A$ we have $A(\widehat{S})\ge A(S^{\circ})$, so
\[
\Delta\varphi(S^{\circ})-\Delta\varphi(\widehat{S})
= \big(A(S^{\circ})-A(\widehat{S})\big) + r(S^{\circ}) - r(\widehat{S})
\;\le\; r(S^{\circ}) - r(\widehat{S}) \;\le\; 2\sup_S|r(S)| .
\]
For the margin condition, suppose $A(\widehat{S})-A(S)>|r(S)|+|r(\widehat{S})|$
for all $S\neq\widehat{S}$. Then
$\Delta\varphi(\widehat S)-\Delta\varphi(S) = A(\widehat S)-A(S) + r(\widehat S)-r(S) > 0$,
so $\widehat S$ is the strict maximiser. For the last clause, let
$S_1,\dots,S_m,S_{m+1}$ be drawn uniformly without replacement from the design
space. The values $|r(S_1)|,\dots,|r(S_{m+1})|$ are exchangeable, so
$|r(S_{m+1})|$ is equally likely to occupy any rank among the $m+1$; it exceeds
all of the first $m$ with probability at most $1/(m+1)$, with equality when ties
have probability zero. \hfill$\square$

Two things this does not say. It does not bound the regret against an adversarial
competitor: the last clause is a statement about a uniformly drawn alternative,
not a supremum over the design space, and a competitor chosen to have a large
remainder is not covered. And it inherits the caveat of
Remark~\ref{rem:vacuous}: the deterministic form is only as useful as the
available control on $\sup_S|r(S)|$, which is why the computable form is the one
we use. Verified in the released harness: the realised regret respects the bound,
the margin condition certifies $75$ of $77$ competitors as dominated with no
false certification, and the exceedance rate of the conformal quantile tracks
$1/(m+1)$ at $m\in\{10,20,40\}$.

\paragraph{What the theorem does not say.}
It does not say the remainder is small at any particular scale, and
Remark~\ref{rem:vacuous} explains why the constant cannot be used numerically.
It does not license using $\sum_{\ell\in S} a^{\varphi}_\ell$ as an estimate of
how much a graft will help; Figure~\ref{fig:verify}(c) shows magnitudes carry
$9$--$29\%$ relative error even where ranking is exact. It assumes the same
architecture and a shared base, and it assumes differentiability, which excludes
hard-routed mixture-of-experts blocks without smoothing.

\section{Standard machinery: optimisers and the drift bound}
\label{app:proof-optimiser}
% ============================================================================

Nothing in this appendix is new. It is collected here so the main text carries
only the results that are, and so that the claims can be checked.

\paragraph{(a) Contiguous windows in $O(L\log L)$.}
Let $U_0=R_0=0$ and $U_b=\sum_{\ell<b}a^{\util}_\ell$, $R_b=\sum_{\ell<b}a^{\risk}_\ell$.
The window $[i,j]$ has value $U_{j+1}-U_i$ and risk $R_{j+1}-R_i$, so the
program is
\begin{equation}
\max_{0\le i < b \le L}\; U_b - U_i
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad R_i \le R_b + \tau .
\end{equation}
Sweeping $b$ upward and inserting $(R_i,U_i)$ for $i=b-1$ before querying, the
inner problem is $\min\{U_i : R_i \le R_b+\tau\}$, a prefix-minimum query over a
key that is known offline. Coordinate-compressing the $L+1$ values of $R$ and
using a Fenwick tree of prefix minima gives $O(\log L)$ per insert and per
query, hence $O(L\log L)$ overall. Correctness is that every feasible window is
enumerated exactly once as a pair $(i,b)$. Agreement with the $O(L^2)$ brute
force on $400$ random instances is reported in Table~\ref{tab:verify}.

\paragraph{(b) Arbitrary subsets.}
With both signs present the program is a $0/1$ knapsack with signed weights,
which is NP-hard in general, so we solve it pseudo-polynomially. Let
$\mathrm{lo}=\sum_{\ell: a^{\risk}_\ell<0}a^{\risk}_\ell$,
$\mathrm{hi}=\sum_{\ell: a^{\risk}_\ell>0}a^{\risk}_\ell$ and put a uniform grid
of $N$ cells of width $h=(\mathrm{hi}-\mathrm{lo})/N$ on $[\mathrm{lo},\mathrm{hi}]$.
Round each weight conservatively: a negative $a^{\risk}_\ell$ is charged
$-h\lceil -a^{\risk}_\ell/h\rceil$, which is at least as negative as the truth,
and a positive one is credited $h\lfloor a^{\risk}_\ell/h\rfloor$, which is at
most the truth. Any set feasible on the grid then satisfies the true constraint,
so the DP never returns a violating solution. Because each of the at most $L$
selected items is mis-stated by less than $h$, the grid feasible set contains
every set with true slack at least $Lh$, so the optimality gap is at most the
best objective attainable in that slack margin and is one-sided. The DP itself
is the standard $O(LN)$ table. On $60$ random instances at $L=12$ checked
against exhaustive $2^{12}$ search there were $0$ violations at every grid
resolution, and the maximum gap fell from $1.8\times10^{-1}$ at $N=200$ to
$0$ at $N=5000$ (Table~\ref{tab:verify}). \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{the drift certificate.}
$\widehat{S}$ is a function of the probe set only, so conditional on
$\widehat{S}$ the calibration indicators $Z_1,\dots,Z_n$ are exchangeable
Bernoulli draws from the deployment population, and
$\sum_i Z_i \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n, \pi)$ with $\pi$ the population drift rate. The
Clopper--Pearson upper limit $\bar{u}_\delta(k,n)$ is by construction the
largest $\pi$ with $\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n,\pi)\le k]>\delta$, hence
$\Pr[\pi\le\bar{u}_\delta(\sum_i Z_i,n)]\ge 1-\delta$. Certifying $K$
candidates at level $\delta/K$ and taking a union bound gives the simultaneous
statement. Simulated coverage at nominal $0.95$ is $0.971$--$1.000$ across the
drift rates tested (Table~\ref{tab:verify}); the conservatism is the known
discreteness of the exact binomial interval. \hfill$\square$

\section{Limitations in full}
\label{app:limits}
% ============================================================================

The linearisation has a regime, mapped in Figure~\ref{fig:verify}(c) and not yet
measured for real experts (E2). The readouts are teacher-forced
log-probabilities while the failure that matters is a property of free
generation; E7(d) measures that gap.
Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}'s constant is exponential in depth and is not
usable numerically (Remark~\ref{rem:vacuous}). The certificate bounds drift under
the calibration distribution, says nothing about task accuracy, and assumes
exchangeability, which fails under shift. Both experts must share an architecture
and a base checkpoint, as in all layer-swap work, and the utility probe relies on
machine translation, which E7(a) ablates. Two negative findings are recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:negative} instead of
dropped: a conjecture that the realised error tracks the donor--host Jacobian gap
at fixed weight distance, tested and refuted, so no claim rests on it; and a
trained-from-scratch transformer testbed in which the transfer phenomenon did not
emerge at all, which constrains E1 to genuinely pretrained multilingual bases.
Finally, all language-model claims here are protocol, not evidence: the numbers
do not exist yet, and if E1 returns a low rank correlation the central claim is
false and the paper should report that.

\section{Negative results and excluded claims}
\label{app:negative}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{A refuted conjecture: the $\lambda$ factor is not predictive.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition} bounds the remainder by a product containing the
donor--host Jacobian gap $\lambda$. That suggested a claim we wanted to make, and
one that would have separated the analysis from a generic Taylor argument: with
the weight perturbation norm held fixed, expert pairs whose Jacobians agree more
closely should show a smaller linearisation error. We tested it directly in the
controlled stack, sampling fourteen donors at a weight distance held constant to
within $0.00\%$ and varying only the direction. The correlation between $\lambda$
and the realised error was $-0.304$, the wrong sign, while weight distance, held
fixed by construction, correlated at $0.000$. The conjecture is not supported and
no claim in this paper rests on it. Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition} is unaffected,
since an upper bound containing $\lambda$ stays valid whether or not $\lambda$ is
tight; what fails is the stronger reading of $\lambda$ as a predictor. The check
is retained in the released harness and reported there as an excluded conjecture
and not as a passing claim.

\paragraph{A testbed that did not reproduce the phenomenon.}
Section~\ref{sec:verify} verifies the mathematics on a stack whose experts are
constructed and not trained, which is a fair criticism to make of it. We
therefore built a small transformer with attention, layer normalisation and GELU
MLPs, wrote its backward pass by hand and gradient-checked it to a worst relative
error of $9\times10^{-9}$, pretrained it on a task whose input script and output
language are decoupled so that a script-independent value representation is
forced, and fine-tuned a skill expert and a language expert from that shared
base. The target cell, the skill in the language nobody was trained on, was left
genuinely unseen. Across two designs of the pretraining task no graft improved on
the host, and the best contiguous window was within noise of the host itself. The
natural inference is not that grafting fails but that the phenomenon needs a base
whose multilingual representations are strong enough to survive expert
fine-tuning, which a small model trained from scratch on a synthetic task does not
have. The practical consequence is a constraint on E1: it should be run on a
genuinely pretrained multilingual base, and a plan that substitutes a small
from-scratch model will fail for reasons that have nothing to do with the
selection method. The testbed is released so that the negative result is
reproducible.

\paragraph{Design axes covered.} Three bases, so no claim rests on one
checkpoint; three languages spanning script and resource level, because the
published gate moves with typological distance; and two task families, because a
method that scores a readout should not care which readout. Every table reports
task accuracy, drift, \emph{and} retained general capability, since a
composition can buy the target behaviour by damaging everything else.

\paragraph{Models.} Tier A: a 1B--2B decoder, where the exhaustive sweep is
affordable and E1 can falsify the paper. Tier B: two 8B decoders from different
families, one of which should be a base for which per-language experts are
already public, so the scale tier does not depend on training them.

\paragraph{Data.} Task family 1 (math): English math experts from
\citep{yu2024metamath, cobbe2021gsm8k}; evaluation on MGSM \citep{shi2022mgsm}.
Task family 2 (safety): an English safety expert and evaluation on a multilingual
jailbreak set, following \citet{shin2026safetyswap}, which also gives a
head-to-head against their published numbers. Language experts: per-language
general instruction data, one base checkpoint, following
\citet{bandarkar2025layerswap}.

\paragraph{Metrics.} \emph{Task}: exact match on MGSM; refusal rate on the safety
set. \emph{Drift}: fraction of generations whose language-identifier label
\citep{grave2018fasttext} is not the target. \emph{Retention}: Belebele-style
reading comprehension and a multilingual knowledge benchmark, to catch a
composition that wins the target metric by breaking the model. \emph{Cost}:
merged models built, forward/backward passes, wall-clock, peak memory.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Three expert-training seeds and five decoding seeds,
reported separately, because the two variances are different and conflating them
hides which one dominates. Paired bootstrap over items for differences, Holm
correction across languages, and confidence intervals on every headline number.

\paragraph{Compute budget.} The plan must be runnable, so it is costed. Tier A
dominates: $L(L{+}1)/2 \approx 300$ merged models, each evaluated on a dev split,
is the single largest line item and is the reason the ground-truth tier is 1B--2B
and not 8B. Estimate this before starting; if the Tier A sweep does not fit in
the available budget, shrink the dev split or the model, and do not silently
substitute a partial sweep for the full one, because E1's value comes from being
exhaustive.

\paragraph{Measured Tier-A E1 gate.}
The exhaustive contiguous sweeps contain 406 windows over 28 layers for each
of Spanish, Chinese, and Swahili. The measured rank correlations are
Spearman $0.991$, $0.985$, and $0.990$, respectively; utility-regret
fractions are $23.6\%$, $14.7\%$, and $88.2\%$. The score-based selection
phase materialized zero merged models, while each exhaustive sweep built 407
models including the host baseline. These values are teacher-forced utility
gate artifacts; accuracy, drift, retention, free-generation, and test-set
measurements remain to be run. The Swahili result triggers the predeclared
regime-investigation stop rule.

\TORUN{\textbf{E2 (regime measurement).} Measure $\varepsilon_S$, $\lambda$ and
the realised remainder for real expert pairs at both tiers and place them on the
axis of Figure~\ref{fig:verify}(c). Also fit the conformal remainder quantile of
Proposition~\ref{prop:regret} at $m\in\{10,20,40\}$ and check its exceedance rate.
\emph{Falsifies the regime claim if:} real pairs sit where rank correlation has
collapsed, in which case the paper reports a limit on applicability.}

\TORUN{\textbf{E3 (gate localisation; the decisive mechanism test).} Per
language, compare the depth at which $a^{\risk}_\ell$ collapses against the swept
drift cliff, against the cross-language weight-delta alignment statistic of
\citet{lasbordes2026rethinking}, and against the specialisation ratio of
\citet{shin2026safetyswap}. \emph{Output:} absolute error in predicted gate depth
per language, and whether each expert-intrinsic statistic separates the
languages at all. \emph{Reduces:} the objection that a cheaper expert-intrinsic
statistic already suffices. \emph{Falsifies the motivation if:} the
specialisation ratio localises the gate as well as the adjoint score does, in
which case the extra machinery is not earning its place and the paper says so.}

\TORUN{\textbf{E4 (baselines).} The primary comparison is the \emph{unlabelled
surrogate sweep}: build every candidate and score it on the same probes with the
same $\varphi_\util,\varphi_\risk$. It needs no labels, so beating the labelled
sweep alone proves nothing; report how closely \textsc{Suture} tracks the
surrogate sweep and at what cost. The remaining baselines are grouped by what
their criterion reads, matching Section~\ref{sec:related}.
\emph{Expert-intrinsic:} task arithmetic \citep{ilharco2023task}, which an
in-the-wild evaluation reports as the one method reliably giving gains on real
checkpoints \citep{hitit2026inthewild}; TIES \citep{yadav2023ties}; DARE
\citep{yu2024dare}; Localize-and-Stitch \citep{he2024localize} and RegMean
\citep{jin2023regmean}, the two strongest performers on MergeBench
\citep{he2025mergebench}; WUDI \citep{cheng2025wudi}; the specialisation-ratio
selector of \citet{shin2026safetyswap}; and a weight-norm selector.
\emph{Behaviour-referenced:} entropy-minimised layer coefficients
\citep{yang2024adamerging} and the activation-statistics coefficients of
\citet{acm2025}. \emph{References:} the published fixed window; a random-window
selector at matched budget over at least $20$ draws, reported as a distribution;
mixed-data fine-tuning, which \citet{bandarkar2025layerswap} use as the
comparison layer swapping must beat; and the labelled sweep as oracle.
\emph{Adaptation note, to be stated in the paper:} most merging methods were
designed to fuse $k$ experts that all have data into one multi-task model,
whereas here two experts are composed for a third cell with no data. Each
baseline's adaptation must be written out explicitly, and any baseline that
cannot be adapted without distorting it should be reported as inapplicable
and not crippled.}

\TORUN{\textbf{E5 (the design space).} Run selection in four spaces at identical
cost: layer intervals, layer subsets, sublayer intervals, sublayer subsets.
Build the winner of each and evaluate. \emph{Output:} accuracy, drift and
retention per space against the best layer interval, plus the models an
enumerating sweep would need for each. Either result is reported: a gain
overturns an assumption every published layer swap makes, and no gain justifies
current practice.}

\TORUN{\textbf{E6 (certificate).} On held-out calibration prompts, check that the
realised drift rate falls under $\bar{u}_\delta$ for
$\delta\in\{0.1,0.05,0.01\}$, report tightness, and repeat with $K=5$ candidates
under the Bonferroni correction. \emph{Also report} coverage when calibration and
test prompts are deliberately drawn from different distributions, which is the
condition under which the guarantee is expected to fail and should be shown
failing.}

\TORUN{\textbf{E7 (ablations).} (a) Probe language: translated target-language
math against English-only math, isolating whether the adjoint transfers across
input language, and removing the machine-translation dependency if English
probes suffice. (b) Probe size $|\mathcal{P}|\in\{8,32,128,512\}$ with the
standard error from \texttt{GraftScores}; find the knee. (c) \textsc{Suture}-IG
at $m\in\{1,4,8\}$. (d) Teacher-forced surrogate against free generation, the
gap flagged in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. (e) Donor identity: reproduce the
published finding that only one source expert helps, and test whether
$a^{\util}$ predicts which. (f) Tolerance $\tau$: trace the accuracy--drift
frontier as $\tau$ varies, which is the curve a practitioner actually chooses on.
(g) Readout choice: two different utility functionals for the same task, to show
the selection is not an artifact of one readout.}

\TORUN{\textbf{E8 (the generalisations).} (a) \emph{Multi-donor:} add a third
expert and score all $3^{L}$ assignments from one pass; build the top assignment
and the best two-expert graft and compare. (b) \emph{Soft coefficients:} sweep
$\alpha$ on the selected units, check that measured $\Delta\varphi$ tracks
$\alpha\sum a_\ell$, and compare the resulting coefficients against
entropy-minimised \citep{yang2024adamerging} and activation-derived
\citep{acm2025} coefficients at matched accuracy, reporting the cost of each.
\emph{Falsifies the generalisation if:} Pearson falls below about $0.6$ on
either.}

\TORUN{\textbf{E9 (second task family; readout-agnosticism).} Repeat E1 and E4
with the safety readout replacing the math readout, on the same experts and the
same code path, changing only $\varphi_\util$. \emph{Supports:} that the method
scores a readout and not a task, which is the claim that makes the framework
more than a layer-swap trick. \emph{Reduces:} the single-task objection.
\emph{Falsifies the framing if:} the method works for math and not for safety
with no explanation of the difference. This experiment also puts the paper
head-to-head with \citet{shin2026safetyswap} on their own setting.}

\TORUN{\textbf{E10 (qualitative failure analysis).} Sample generations from the
selected model, the sweep optimum and the worst window, in each language.
Categorise the failures: language drift, arithmetic error, degenerate repetition,
refusal. \emph{Supports:} that the drift metric measures what it claims.
\emph{Reduces:} the objection that automatic language identification is a proxy
nobody has looked behind.}

\section{Notes for the experimental tier}
\label{app:notes}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Streaming the donor.}
Line 4 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:suture} needs the donor's unit $\ell$ only while
unit $\ell$ is being scored, so peak memory is the host plus one unit rather
than two full models. On a shared base, storing the donor as a task vector
further reduces the footprint.

\paragraph{Cost accounting.}
Let $|\mathcal{P}|$ be the probe count, $C_f$ the cost of a host forward pass,
and $C_b$ a backward pass. Selection costs $|\mathcal{P}|(2C_f + C_b)$ and zero
model builds. An exhaustive contiguous sweep costs
$\tfrac{L(L+1)}{2}\left(C_{\mathrm{build}} + C_{\mathrm{bench}}\right)$ where
$C_{\mathrm{bench}}$ is a full generative benchmark run. The ratio is reported
as measured wall-clock in Table~\ref{tab:main}; the point of the accounting is
that the left side does not depend on the number of candidate windows.

\paragraph{Released artifact.}
Exhaustive sweeps of the kind E1 requires do not currently exist in public form,
which is why every paper re-runs one. We will release the full sweep tables,
accuracy and drift for all $L(L+1)/2$ windows per model and language, as a
reference set against which any future graft-selection method can be scored
without re-paying the sweep.
