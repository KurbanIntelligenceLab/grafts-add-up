################################################################################
#  READ THESE THREE THINGS BEFORE YOU RUN ANYTHING ELSE                        #
################################################################################

(1) THE ADJOINT AND THE INJECTION MUST BE EVALUATED ON THE **SAME** HOST
    TRAJECTORY, AND THE PREDICTION AND THE GROUND TRUTH MUST BE AVERAGED OVER
    THE **SAME** PROBE SET.

    a_l = E_p < s_l(p) , v_l(p) >   where BOTH s_l and v_l come from the host's
    forward pass on the SAME prompt p. If you cache adjoints from one batch and
    injections from another, or average the score over 16 prompts and compare
    it against a merged model measured on 1 prompt, the correlation collapses
    and you will wrongly conclude the method does not work.

    This exact mistake was made while writing the reference implementation. It
    produced Spearman 0.38 instead of 0.93 on a fixture where the mathematics
    is exact. It looks like a negative result. It is a bookkeeping bug.
    Sanity check before trusting anything: score a SINGLE prompt, build the
    merged model, measure that SINGLE prompt, and confirm agreement first.

(2) DO NOT BUILD A MERGED MODEL DURING SELECTION. IF YOU DO, THE PAPER'S CLAIM
    IS GONE.

    The entire contribution is that selection costs two forward passes and one
    backward pass over the HOST, and zero merged models. It is very tempting,
    when debugging, to "just check" by materialising a few merged models and
    peeking at benchmark scores, then quietly letting that influence the
    tolerance tau or the probe design. That is the labelled sweep wearing a
    disguise, and it invalidates the cost claim and the certificate's
    independence. Keep a hard wall:
        probe set   -> scores -> selection      (no benchmark, no labels)
        calibration -> certificate              (no labels, disjoint prompts)
        test set    -> reported numbers         (touched ONCE, at the end)
    Log every merged model you ever build, with a reason. If the log has more
    entries than E1 and E5 require, say so in the paper.

(3) USE A GENUINELY PRETRAINED MULTILINGUAL BASE. THIS IS NOT A PREFERENCE.

    We built a small transformer, trained it from scratch, fine-tuned two
    experts from it, and tried to reproduce layer swapping in a system where
    the exhaustive sweep is ground truth. It did not reproduce. Across two
    designs of the pretraining task, NO graft improved on the host. Code:
    verify/toy_transfer.py; write-up: Appendix D of the paper.

    The inference is not that grafting fails. It is that the phenomenon needs a
    base whose multilingual representations are strong enough to survive expert
    fine-tuning, and a small from-scratch model does not have that. If you
    substitute a model you train yourself to save compute, you will get a null
    result for reasons that have nothing to do with the selection method, and
    you will spend weeks concluding the wrong thing.

MODELS
  Tier A (ground truth, run first): any 1B-2B open decoder with a multilingual
    tokenizer. L is typically 16-28, so the exhaustive contiguous sweep is
    L(L+1)/2 = 136-406 merged models. That is affordable at this scale and it
    is what makes Tier A the tier that can falsify the paper.
  Tier B (scale check): an 8B open decoder. Prefer a base for which public
    per-language experts already exist, so you do not pay for expert training.
    >>> VERIFY AVAILABILITY: the long-CoT layer-swap paper (arXiv 2605.26735)
    states it releases per-language specialists on a public model hub. If those
    checkpoints are genuinely downloadable, Tier B costs almost nothing and you
    additionally get a PUBLISHED window sweep to check your prediction against.
    Confirm the licence and the checkpoint list before planning around this.

EXPERTS (Tier A, you train these)
  Donor (skill): English math SFT. MetaMathQA and/or GSM8K train split.
  Host (language): target-language general instruction data. One host per
    language.
  Train both from the SAME base checkpoint with the SAME hyperparameters. If
  the bases differ by even a revision, every claim in the paper is void.

LANGUAGES: pick three that span the axis the published gate shift moves along:
  - one high-resource Latin-script language
  - one high-resource non-Latin-script language
  - one lower-resource language
Prior work reports the gate location moving with typological distance, so a
three-language spread is the minimum that can test E3.

PROBE AND CALIBRATION SETS (all label-free; build these before any run)
  P_util : machine-translate GSM8K **TRAIN** questions into the target
           language. Gold answer = the numeric answer from the English source.
           No annotator. 200-500 items.
  P_risk : target-language passages paired with machine translations into the
           donor's language. 200-500 items.
  C      : held-out target-language prompts, DISJOINT from P_util and P_risk,
           used only for the certificate. 500-1000 items.
  TEST   : MGSM target-language test split. Touch once.
  Assert disjointness in code, by hash. Do not eyeball it.

################################################################################
#  1.  CHEAP PILOT  (do this before any full experiment; ~1 GPU-hour)
################################################################################

Purpose: catch bookkeeping bugs while they are cheap.

P1. Take the Tier A base. Make a FAKE donor by adding small random noise to the
    host's weights. Compute scores on 8 prompts. Build 10 random merged models.
    Check predicted vs measured on those SAME 8 prompts.
    EXPECT: Spearman > 0.9. This tests your plumbing, not the science, because
    a random donor has a tiny Jacobian gap and must be in the linear regime.
    IF THIS FAILS, STOP. The bug is in your adapter, not in the paper.

P2. Repeat with the real donor on 8 prompts and 10 merged models.
    EXPECT: nothing in particular. This is the first real measurement. Record
    Spearman, Pearson, relative L2 error, eps_S and lambda. This single number
    tells you which regime you are in before you spend anything.

P3. Verify the adjoint numerically on ONE layer: perturb x_{l+1} by a small
    random delta, measure the change in phi, compare against < s_l, delta >.
    EXPECT: agreement to ~1e-4 relative for a small delta. If not, your
    backward pass is not returning d phi / d x_{l+1}.

Do not proceed past P3 until all three behave.

################################################################################
#  2.  EXPERIMENTS
################################################################################

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1  POSITIVE CONTROL -- RUN THIS FIRST. IT CAN FALSIFY THE PAPER.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective : Does the first-order score rank REAL layer grafts?
Tier      : A (1B-2B), one language to start, then all three.
Data      : P_util, P_risk for scoring; MGSM dev (NOT test) for the sweep.
Method    : (a) exhaustive contiguous sweep: build all L(L+1)/2 merged models,
                score MGSM-dev accuracy and drift rate on each;
            (b) run SUTURE once; predict every window from the 2L scores.
Baselines : (see E4; E1 is against the sweep itself, which is the oracle)
Metrics   : Spearman(predicted, swept) over all windows; accuracy regret of the
            SUTURE-selected window against the sweep-optimal window; top-5
            window overlap; wall-clock for each route; number of merged models
            built by each route.
Seeds     : 3 seeds for expert training, 5 decode seeds for MGSM. Report mean
            and sd. Paired bootstrap over items for the regret.
Expected  : If the claim holds, high Spearman and near-zero regret at roughly
            two orders of magnitude less compute.
Supports  : the central claim of the paper.
Reduces   : "first-order scores will not rank real grafts".
STOP RULE : Spearman below ~0.5 or regret a large fraction of the achievable
            gain means the central claim is FALSE at this scale. Do not tune
            the probe set until it passes. Report it, and check E2 to see
            whether the regime explains it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E2  REGIME MEASUREMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective : Are real expert pairs inside the linearisation's usable regime?
Method    : Measure eps_S, lambda (or a cheap proxy: ||J_d - J_h|| estimated by
            a few random JVP probes) and the realised remainder for real expert
            pairs at both tiers. Place them on the axis of Figure 2(c).
Metrics   : Spearman and relative L2 error of predicted vs measured, as a
            function of graft size and of fine-tuning amount (checkpoint at
            several training steps to sweep the scale).
Supports  : the honesty of Section 5; explains any E1 result either way.
Note      : a NEGATIVE answer here is publishable and must be reported as a
            limit on applicability, not buried.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E3  GATE LOCALISATION AND THE CROSS-LANGUAGE SHIFT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective : Is the language gate an adjoint property rather than a weight-space
            property?
Method    : For each language, plot a^r_l against depth. Compare the depth at
            which it collapses to (i) the swept drift cliff from E1, and (ii)
            the cross-language weight-delta alignment statistic used in prior
            work (mean pairwise cosine of per-language deltas, and top-1 SVD
            variance share).
Metrics   : absolute error in predicted gate depth, per language; whether the
            weight-space statistic distinguishes the languages at all.
Supports  : that SUTURE is not rediscovering a cheaper statistic.
Reduces   : "a weight-norm heuristic would do the same".
Note      : this is the highest-upside and highest-risk experiment. Prior work
            reports a gate that moves between languages while its weight-space
            statistic does not. If a^r predicts the shift, that is a finding of
            independent interest. If it does not, say so plainly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E4  BASELINES  -- THE PRIMARY ONE IS NOT THE LABELLED SWEEP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BASELINE SET IS GROUPED BY WHAT THE CRITERION READS, which is the paper's spine.
Do not ship a 2023-era baseline set: on MergeBench (He et al. 2025), the
strongest merging methods for domain-specialised LLMs are Localize-and-Stitch
and RegMean, with TA and TIES mid-tier and DARE and Fisher lower. An in-the-wild
evaluation additionally reports Task Arithmetic as the only method reliably
giving gains on real heterogeneous checkpoints, so TA is a serious baseline and
not a formality.

ADAPTATION IS THE FAIRNESS ISSUE. Most merging methods were designed to fuse k
experts that ALL have data into one multi-task model. Here two experts are
composed for a THIRD cell with no data. Write out each baseline's adaptation
explicitly in the paper. If a method cannot be adapted without distorting it,
report it as inapplicable rather than crippling it and claiming a win.
THE COMPARISON THAT DECIDES E4 is the UNLABELLED SURROGATE SWEEP: build every
candidate model and score it with the same phi_util and phi_risk on the same
probes. It needs no labels either, so beating only the labelled sweep proves
nothing and a reviewer will say so. Report how closely SUTURE tracks the
surrogate sweep and at what cost ratio (about C/3 for C candidates; the real
claim is that SUTURE is constant in C while the sweep is linear in it).

Then compare, on the same experts, same test sets, same seeds:
    host expert alone; donor expert alone;
    published fixed heuristic (top/bottom-k from the language expert);
    task arithmetic; TIES; DARE;
    layer-wise coefficients by entropy minimisation on unlabelled target text;
    weight-norm selector (pick the window with the largest ||delta||);
    random window at matched budget (>= 20 draws, report the distribution);
    full labelled sweep (oracle upper bound);
    SUTURE; SUTURE-IG.
Report accuracy, drift rate, AND selection cost (merged models built +
wall-clock) for every row. The random-window distribution is the one reviewers
will ask for; include it from the start.
NOTE ON THE ENTROPY BASELINE: it needs a signal at the target cell, which by
construction does not exist here. Run it anyway and report what it does. If it
degenerates, that is a result supporting Section 2, not an excuse to omit it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E5  THE DESIGN SPACE -- THE EXPERIMENT THIS PAPER EXISTS TO MAKE POSSIBLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective : Every prior paper grafts a contiguous band of whole layers, because
            a sweep has to enumerate. Neither restriction has been tested.
Method    : Run selection in FOUR spaces at identical cost, and build the winner
            of each:
              (i)   layer intervals          (what everyone does)
              (ii)  layer subsets            (drops contiguity)
              (iii) sublayer intervals       (attn and MLP scored separately)
              (iv)  sublayer subsets         (2^(2L); a sweep cannot enumerate)
Metrics   : accuracy and drift per space against the best layer interval, plus
            the number of models an enumerating sweep would need for each.
Expected  : unknown. In the controlled stack the subset optimum beat the best
            interval by 82%, which shows the gain is possible in principle and
            nothing more. A gain here overturns an assumption in all prior work;
            no gain justifies current practice and is a clean negative.
Note      : if E1 is only a modest win over the surrogate sweep, E5 is what
            carries the paper. Do not skip it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E6  CERTIFICATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective : Does the drift bound hold, and is it tight enough to be useful?
Method    : On C (disjoint from P), generate with the selected model, label
            each generation with the language identifier, apply
            certify_drift(). Repeat for delta in {0.1, 0.05, 0.01}. Then repeat
            certifying K = 5 candidate grafts with the Bonferroni correction.
Metrics   : realised drift rate on TEST vs the bound computed on C; coverage
            across languages and seeds; bound width.
Supports  : Proposition 4.
Watch for : exchangeability. If C and TEST come from different distributions
            the guarantee does not transfer, and you must say so.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E7  ABLATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) Probe language: translated target-language math vs English-only math.
    Isolates whether the adjoint transfers across input language. If English
    probes work as well, the method gets cheaper and the translation caveat in
    the limitations section goes away.
(b) Probe size: score stability vs |P| at 8, 32, 128, 512. Report the SEM from
    GraftScores. Find the knee.
(c) SUTURE-IG at m in {1, 4, 8}. m=1 is plain SUTURE.
(d) Teacher-forced surrogate vs free generation. THIS IS THE ONE A REVIEWER
    WILL PRESS ON. The readouts are teacher-forced log-probabilities; the
    failure that matters is a property of free generation. Measure the
    correlation between the two directly.
(e) Donor identity: prior work reports that only one source expert helps.
    Reproduce that, and test whether a^u predicts which donor helps.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E9  SECOND TASK FAMILY  -- READOUT-AGNOSTICISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective : Does the method score a READOUT or a TASK? If only math works, the
            framework claim collapses to a layer-swap trick.
Method    : Repeat E1 and E4 with a safety readout replacing the math readout,
            on the same experts and the SAME CODE PATH. Change only phi_util.
Metrics   : refusal rate on a multilingual jailbreak set, plus drift and
            retention, following Shin & Hwang so the comparison is head-to-head
            with their published numbers on their own setting.
Falsifies : the framing, if math works and safety does not with no explanation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E10 QUALITATIVE FAILURE ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective : Is the drift metric measuring drift, or is fastText a proxy nobody
            has looked behind?
Method    : Sample generations from the selected model, the sweep optimum and
            the worst window, per language. Categorise: language drift,
            arithmetic error, degenerate repetition, refusal.
Note      : reviewers ask for this and it is cheap. Do not skip it.

################################################################################
#  3.  FLAG BACK IMMEDIATELY IF
################################################################################
- P1 (fake donor) gives Spearman < 0.9. Plumbing bug; do not proceed.
- E1 Spearman < 0.5. The central claim may be false. Stop and reassess the
  framing before spending on E4-E7.
- E2 puts real experts far outside the usable regime. The paper's scope must
  shrink to that regime, explicitly, in the abstract.
- The certificate is violated on TEST. Check the disjointness assertions and
  the exchangeability assumption first; then report it.
- Any merged model gets built during selection for any reason. Log it.
- Retention drops on any configuration you are about to report as a win. A
  composition that buys the target metric by damaging general capability is not
  a win, and reporting it as one is the easiest way to fail review.
- A number changes between runs without a corresponding code or seed change.
  Stop and diff the working tree; do not re-run until it is explained.
- Bibliography: any entry still marked AUTHORS UNVERIFIED at submission time.
- ANY citation rendering with a surname nobody on the team recognises. Two
  fabricated author lists were inserted into this bundle's .bib during
  preparation, under comments claiming verifications that never happened. See
  REDFLAGS.txt. Render the PDF and read the bibliography as it renders; do not
  trust the .bib source.
