\title{\bf Grafts Add Up: First-Order Scoring of Layer Compositions}
\author{Anonymous authors}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Layer swapping composes two experts fine-tuned from a shared base by replacing a
band of transformer layers in one with the corresponding layers of the other,
moving task ability into languages that have no task data. Which layers to swap is
found by a sweep: build a merged model per candidate, score it, and retain the
best, repeating the procedure for each base, language, and task. Existing low-cost
criteria read the experts alone and score one configuration at a time; their
aggregate rank correlations can fail on the candidates that matter. Under the
stated smoothness assumptions, we show that graft effects are additive across
units to first order, with a remainder that is second order in the fine-tuning
scale.
Contracting with the adjoint of a chosen readout turns those injections into $L$
numbers per readout, obtained from one host forward--backward sweep plus streamed
donor-block evaluations, without building candidate merged models during
selection. The resulting $2L$ values assign first-order scores to all
$2^{L}$ single-donor compositions; selection under a behavioural constraint is a
constrained maximum-subarray problem in $O(L\log L)$, and the same decomposition
extends to arbitrary subsets, module granularity, and several donors. Because the
score is the leading term of an identity rather than a heuristic, it also supports
margin-based comparison with sampled or explicitly evaluated competitors.

Where exhaustive search provides ground truth, the scores rank candidate bands at
Spearman $0.95\pm0.05$ and the sampled-competitor test certifies $65\pm10$ of
$77$ competitors as dominated from $11$ built models against the sweep's $78$.
Both factors are necessary: dropping either
raises selection regret from $0.03$ to about $0.25$, and per-layer
parameter-change magnitude, the statistic from which the original band was
chosen, reaches $0.29$ against $0.33$ for random scores.

\textbf{An archived B3 cloud check on public Qwen3-8B specialists} reports
selected--host gains of $+0.398$ and $+1.326$ nats/token in French and Chinese
under teacher-forced answer log-probability; it compares three candidates rather
than a full-window or free-generation ranking.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================

Composing a skill expert with a language expert by swapping transformer layers
is a post hoc recipe that has been used to move a capability into a
language with no task data (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). \citet{bandarkar2025layerswap} fine-tune an English
math expert and a target-language general expert from a shared base, replace the
top and bottom blocks of the math expert with those of the language expert, and
report gains on a multilingual math benchmark. The recipe has since been
reused in two further settings: transferring long chain-of-thought reasoning
into native-language reasoners \citep{lasbordes2026rethinking}, and
transferring English safety behaviour into other languages
\citep{shin2026safetyswap}.

All three locate the band the same way: build a merged model per candidate
window and score it on a labelled target-language benchmark. The band does not
transfer. Reported windows are the bottom and top blocks of a 32-block model
\citep{bandarkar2025layerswap}, a mid-stack window of a 36-block reasoning model
narrowed by two blocks for one of five languages \citep{lasbordes2026rethinking},
and different counts for two 8B safety bases \citep{shin2026safetyswap}. The
sweep is repeated for every base, language, and task. Nothing forces it to use
labels: scoring each built model on the inexpensive unlabelled surrogate defined
below is the baseline we compare against throughout. It still builds one model
per candidate, so cost is linear in the candidate count, and it is that
dependence, not the labels, that we remove.

The sweep also guards against a failure that is discrete, not gradual.
\citet{lasbordes2026rethinking} report that extending the window past a
particular block sends roughly 60\% of generated reasoning traces back to
English, and that the block at which this happens moves between languages. Both
statistics they use to motivate the mid-stack window measure agreement among the
per-language fine-tuning updates and are aggregated across all six languages, so
by construction neither can say where the boundary falls for any one of them,
and neither fixes the two endpoints they end up using. Those came from a sweep.
These observations leave the per-language boundary unexplained by the cited
weight-space statistics.

The expert-only criteria discussed in Section~\ref{sec:related} return one
ranking of units, regardless of which behaviour is imported or preserved. We
score instead by the predicted change
in a chosen readout, a property of the experts \emph{and} the behaviour, which is
what makes a capability to import and a behaviour to keep expressible as two
objectives and a hard constraint rather than one ordering.

\paragraph{Grafts add up.} Reading the behaviour would be no less expensive than a sweep
if the effect of grafting a set of units had to be measured set by set. It does
not have to be. The deviation
a graft induces at the end of the stack \emph{adds up}: it is a sum of per-unit
injections, each the disagreement between the two experts' units evaluated on the
\emph{host's own} trajectory, each propagated by the host's Jacobians, with a
remainder that is second order in the fine-tuning scale
(Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}). Because the injections sit on a trajectory the
host produces anyway, they are read off one forward pass, the propagators are read
off one backward pass, and no merged model is built during scoring. Each unit's contribution
is then computed as though no other unit had been grafted, so a graft set is a
subset sum: $2L$ numbers score all $2^{L}$ compositions, and the size of the
design space stops entering the cost of scoring it. The same numbers cover
arbitrary subsets, module granularity and several donors, spaces a sweep cannot
enumerate at all.

Additivity also gives a guarantee unavailable to a heuristic proxy. Because the
score is the leading term of an identity with a bounded remainder, a selection can
be \emph{certified} against a named, evaluated competitor or against a sampled
competitor whose remainder is measured. This is a per-instance comparison, not a
simultaneous guarantee over every unbuilt candidate; conventional merging
criteria are instead defended by aggregate rank correlation against an oracle
sweep.

Concretely, this paper contributes the following.
\textbf{(i) Grafts add up.} The deviation a graft induces in the residual stream
decomposes into per-unit injections propagated by host Jacobians, with a
remainder second order in the fine-tuning scale
(Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}). Because the injection sits on the host's own
trajectory, the selection program becomes linear in the indicator of $S$: $2L$
numbers score all $2^{L}$ compositions and the candidate count never enters the
cost.
\textbf{(ii) Selection can be certified, not merely correlated.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:regret} shows a selector is governed by the score
\emph{margin} against the remainder, not by predictive accuracy, which
gives a per-instance test that a named competitor cannot beat the selection, and
a form costing $m$ built models instead of the whole design space.
\textbf{(iii) The regime is measured and its boundary reported.} On a stack small
enough to enumerate, both factors of the score turn out to be necessary, the
criterion the layer-swap literature uses to locate its band places a window no better
than random, and the point at which the linearisation stops being reliable is
measured and not assumed (Section~\ref{sec:verify}). The low-power evaluations
stopped at pre-specified gates; a separate A100 check gives positive
teacher-forced candidate comparisons in French and Chinese, but no full-window
or free-generation ranking claim (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\section{Setup: graft selection as a constrained program}
\label{sec:setup}
% ============================================================================

Before the scores, the problem they solve. Grafting has two sides that pull
against each other, a capability to import and a surface behaviour to keep, and
writing both down is what the rest of the paper needs.
Let a decoder apply $L$ residual units to a state
$x_\ell \in \Rb^{T\times d}$,
\begin{equation}
x_{\ell+1} \;=\; x_\ell + B_\ell(x_\ell;\theta_\ell), \qquad \ell = 0,\dots,L-1,
\end{equation}
with embedding and unembedding fixed. A host expert $\theta^{\host}$ and a
donor expert $\theta^{\donor}$ are fine-tuned independently from a shared base
$\theta^{\base}$. Following the setting of \citet{bandarkar2025layerswap}, the
host is the expert whose surface behaviour we want to keep and the donor is the
expert whose internal capability we want to import; in the cross-lingual case
the host speaks the target language and the donor does the reasoning.

For $S\subseteq\{0,\dots,L-1\}$, the \emph{graft} $\theta^{S}$ takes donor
parameters at units in $S$ and host parameters elsewhere; layer swapping is the
case where $S$ is a contiguous interval of whole transformer layers.

Nothing below uses the fact that a unit is a whole layer: the development holds
for any partition into sequentially applied units, so $\ell$ may index attention
and MLP sublayers separately. Only the design-space size changes with the
partition; our cost does not.

Two scalar readouts are evaluated on the final state. The \emph{utility}
$\varphi_\util$ measures the imported capability and the \emph{risk}
$\varphi_\risk$ measures retention of the host's surface behaviour. Both are
differentiable teacher-forced log-probability functionals, defined in
Section~\ref{sec:method} so that neither requires human annotation. We use
\emph{risk} for this retention readout; it is not a calibrated failure
probability. Writing
$\Delta\varphi(S) = \Ex_p[\varphi(x_L^{S}(p)) - \varphi(x_L^{\host}(p))]$, the
selection problem is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:program}
\max_{S\in\mathcal{S}} \;\Delta\varphi_\util(S)
\qquad \text{subject to} \qquad
\Delta\varphi_\risk(S) \;\ge\; -\tau ,
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{S}$ the intervals or the full power set. Current practice solves
\eqref{eq:program} by evaluating the objective at each candidate, materialising a
merged model for each. When the objective is a target-language benchmark this
also needs labels in a language that by assumption has none, though the surrogate
readouts below remove that second cost without touching the first. The constraint
is enforced informally, by inspecting generated samples. In the experiments,
\emph{unit} denotes a general sequential primitive, \emph{layer} denotes a
transformer layer, \emph{window} denotes a contiguous interval, and \emph{graft
set} denotes an arbitrary subset.

\paragraph{Relation to unlabelled merging objectives.}
Methods tuning merging coefficients on unlabelled target data
\citep{yang2024adamerging, symerge2026} assume a usable signal at the target
cell. Here neither expert can perform the task in the target language, so there
is no competent teacher, and they backpropagate through the merged model, which
restores the per-candidate cost we are removing.

% ============================================================================
\section{Grafts add up}
\label{sec:theory}
% ============================================================================

Evaluating the objective of \eqref{eq:program} directly is what costs a model per
candidate. The key is to exploit its structure rather than its values, and the
structure is additive.
Fix a prompt $p$ and let $x^{\host}_\ell$ be the host trajectory. Define the
deviation $\Delta_\ell = x^{S}_\ell - x^{\host}_\ell$, with $\Delta_0 = 0$.

The analysis turns on the \emph{injection}, the disagreement between the two
experts' units \emph{on the host's own trajectory},
\begin{equation}
\label{def:injection}
v_\ell \;:=\; B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\donor}_\ell\right) - B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\host}_\ell\right).
\end{equation}

The injection is the object that reduces cost: it depends only on the
host trajectory and the donor's block function, so all $L$ injections are
obtained during a single host forward pass by evaluating each donor block
off-path at the state the host produced.

\begin{assumption}
\label{ass:smooth}
On a neighbourhood of the trajectories, each unit map $x\mapsto B_\ell(x;\theta)$
is Lipschitz with Lipschitz Jacobian, uniformly over the host and every donor
parameter in play, and the donor--host Jacobian gap there is at most $\lambda$.
Appendix~\ref{app:proof-superposition} names the constants and the radius
condition they must jointly satisfy. Nothing here constrains behaviour in
$\theta$; the one result that needs that states its own hypothesis.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Graft superposition]
\label{thm:superposition}
Let $P_{\ell+1:L} = \prod_{k=L-1}^{\ell+1}\!\left(I + J^{\host}_k\right)$ be the
host propagator. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:smooth}, for every $S$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:superposition}
\Delta_L(S) \;=\; \sum_{\ell\in S} P_{\ell+1:L}\, v_\ell \;+\; R(S),
\qquad
\|R(S)\| \;=\; O\!\left(\varepsilon_S(\lambda + \varepsilon_S)\right),
\qquad
\varepsilon_S = \sum_{\ell\in S}\|v_\ell\| .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The sum works because $v_\ell$ is evaluated on the host's \emph{own} trajectory:
each unit's contribution is computed independently of the other grafted units,
and $R(S)$ captures the interactions omitted by this linearisation. Both of its
factors vanish with a
fine-tuning scale $t$ when $\lambda=O(t)$ and $\varepsilon_S=O(t)$ together, and
that is the regime Figure~\ref{fig:verify}(b) measures. Enlarging the graft set at
fixed $\lambda$ grows only $\varepsilon_S$, and the exponent falls to near one. The explicit constant is exponential in depth
(Appendix~\ref{app:proof-superposition}). We therefore use the \emph{order} of
the remainder and never its magnitude. No guarantee in this paper rests on that
constant, and Section~\ref{sec:verify} measures the remainder directly instead of
bounding it.

Contracting \eqref{eq:superposition} with the gradient of a readout turns the
propagated injections into scalars that can be read off a backward pass.

\begin{corollary}[One-pass scoring of every graft set]
\label{cor:scoring}
Let $\varphi$ be differentiable with Lipschitz gradient and define the adjoint
and the per-layer score
\begin{equation}
s^{\varphi}_\ell \;:=\; \left(P_{\ell+1:L}\right)^{\!\top}\nabla\varphi\!\left(x^{\host}_L\right) \;=\; \frac{\partial \varphi\!\left(x^{\host}_L\right)}{\partial x_{\ell+1}},
\qquad
a^{\varphi}_\ell \;:=\; \Ex_p\big\langle s^{\varphi}_\ell(p),\, v_\ell(p)\big\rangle .
\end{equation}
Then $\Delta\varphi(S) = \sum_{\ell\in S} a^{\varphi}_\ell + O\!\left(\varepsilon_S(\lambda+\varepsilon_S)\right)$
for every $S$. With donors $1,\dots,k$ sharing the base the same identity holds
with $\lambda=\max_j\lambda_j$.
\end{corollary}

Because $a^{\varphi}_\ell$ averages a per-prompt identity, the constants of
Assumption~\ref{ass:smooth} are required to hold uniformly over the probe
distribution, and $\varepsilon_S$ is read as $\sup_p\varepsilon_S(p)$.

One backward pass through the host produces every $s^{\varphi}_\ell$; one
off-path evaluation of each donor block at the cached host states produces every
$v_\ell$. Their inner products are $L$ numbers per readout, and every graft set
is a subset sum of them, so the objective and the constraint in
\eqref{eq:program} are linear in the indicator of $S$ and all $2^L$ sets are
scored without instantiating any. Score acquisition is linear in $k$ and
independent of the $(k{+}1)^L$ assignment count, so multi-donor routing costs no
more to score than a single swap. Continuous interpolation between the two
experts needs a further hypothesis, on smoothness in $\theta$ rather than in $x$,
and is treated in Appendix~\ref{app:proof-superposition}; nothing below uses it.

\begin{proposition}[Selection margin]
\label{prop:regret}
Write $A(S)=\sum_{\ell\in S}a^{\varphi}_\ell$ for the first-order score and
$\Delta\varphi(S)=A(S)+r(S)$ for the truth, and let $\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{S}$
be the comparison set (the risk-feasible candidates in a constrained problem).
Let $\widehat{S}\in\arg\max_{S\in\mathcal{F}} A(S)$ be the selection. If
$A(\widehat{S})-A(S) > |r(S)|+|r(\widehat{S})|$ then $\widehat{S}$ beats $S$; if
this holds for every $S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{\widehat{S}\}$ then
$\widehat{S}$ is the true optimum over $\mathcal{F}$. Further, if $m$ candidates
are drawn uniformly without replacement from $\mathcal{F}\setminus\{\widehat{S}\}$
and built, and $q$ is the largest realised $|r|$ among them, then a fresh uniform
draw from $\mathcal{F}\setminus\{\widehat{S}\}$ satisfies
$\Pr[\,|r|>q\,]\le 1/(m+1)$. The regret against the true optimum in
$\mathcal{F}$ is at most $r(S^{\circ})-r(\widehat{S})$; proofs in
Appendix~\ref{app:proof-superposition}.
\end{proposition}

A selector does not need accurate values, only the right ordering, and this is
what says when the ordering is safe: scores carrying tens of percent of magnitude
error can still select exactly, which is what Section~\ref{sec:verify} observes.
The last clause makes the test deployable. Build $\widehat{S}$, which is built
anyway, and $m$ uniformly drawn competitors from
$\mathcal{F}\setminus\{\widehat{S}\}$; with $q$ the
largest realised $|r|$ among those $m$, certify a sampled $S$ whenever
$A(\widehat{S})-A(S)>q+|r(\widehat{S})|$. The guarantee is \emph{per competitor}:
a uniformly drawn $S$ satisfies $|r(S)|\le q$ with probability at least
$m/(m{+}1)$. It does not hold simultaneously over $\mathcal{F};$ the expected
number of the $|\mathcal{F}|-m-1$ remaining competitors with $|r|>q$ is
$(|\mathcal{F}|-m-1)/(m{+}1)$. The certification count should therefore be read as
a sampled-competitor guarantee, not as a guarantee over every candidate.
Either way the test needs $m$ built models, against the displayed condition,
which needs $|r(S)|$ for every $S$ and therefore the entire sweep.
For every reported count, candidates are scored before materialisation; a named
or sampled graft is then built once and
$r(S):=\Delta\varphi(S)-A(S)$ is measured with the same protocol. This is not a
rank-correlation or a worst-case claim over unbuilt candidates.

\section{\textsc{Suture}: turning the scores into a selection}
\label{sec:method}
% ============================================================================

\textsc{Suture} (Sensitivity Under Transfer: Utility and Risk Estimation) applies
the scores of Corollary~\ref{cor:scoring} to probes that need no target-language
annotation, followed by a constrained solve. Maximising
$A(S)$ over contiguous windows under a risk bound is a constrained
maximum-subarray problem, exact in $O(L\log L)$; over arbitrary subsets it is a
signed-weight knapsack with conservative rounding, so it never returns a
violating set. Both are standard and proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:proof-optimiser}. What matters is that they run on $2L$ numbers
and never touch a model: the contribution is what makes the scores available, not
the solve that consumes them.

\paragraph{Certifying the model that is built.}
Scores select; they do not guarantee. If the calibration observations are
i.i.d.\ prompt--seed draws independent of the probes, a Clopper--Pearson upper
limit \citep{clopper1934} bounds the binomial drift rate on the one model built.
The construction is textbook \citep{vovk2005, angelopoulos2023gentle}; it is
affordable only because selection no longer consumes the evaluation budget.
The Spanish run applied it post hoc, not prospectively. Sample sizes and the
i.i.d.\ caveat are in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

\paragraph{Probe sets carry no target-language human annotation.}
$\mathcal{P}_\util$ machine-translates the \emph{training} split of GSM8K
\citep{cobbe2021gsm8k}; the gold answer is a number, so supervision is inherited
from the source. $\mathcal{P}_\risk$ pairs each target-language passage with its
translation into the donor's language. Both are length-normalised teacher-forced
log-probabilities, disjoint from every evaluation benchmark
(Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{Cost, against the strongest baseline.}
The relevant comparison is the unlabelled surrogate sweep: build each candidate
and score it on the same probes. Score acquisition uses the probe set once, with
no candidate builds; the sweep grows with $N_{\mathrm{cand}}$, reaching
$2^{72}$ at sublayer granularity on a 36-layer model. Host states are cached and
donor blocks discarded (Appendix~\ref{app:notes}).

\paragraph{When the linearisation is not enough.}
First-order attribution degrades when the readout saturates or the perturbation
is large \citep{syed2024attribution, kramar2024atp}; averaging
$\langle s_\ell, v_\ell\rangle$ over points along the graft path
\citep{hanna2024eapig} is the standard mitigation and costs a constant factor,
still independent of the design-space size. We did not need it here: selection
uses only the \emph{ranking}, which survives past where magnitudes do
(Section~\ref{sec:verify}).

\STATE {\bfseries return} $\theta^{\widehat{S}}$ and, under i.i.d.\ prompt--seed calibration, $\bar{u}_\delta(k, |\mathcal{C}|)$
\end{algorithmic}

\section{What the controlled stack establishes}
\label{sec:verify}
% ============================================================================

That claim, and the identities it rests on, can be checked where exhaustive
search is affordable: a residual stack with $L=12$ blocks, $d=24$, $T=5$, host
and donor perturbations of a shared base, and a two-token readout. This answers
three questions only. Do the identities hold? Does the score need both factors,
and does it beat the criteria the literature uses? Where does the linearisation
stop? Numbers below are from the released harness, except those quoted from
Figure~\ref{fig:verify}. This is not language-model evidence: the ground truth
here \emph{is} the readout the score is built from.

\paragraph{What the score has to contain.}
The identities hold: predicted and measured change agree over random graft sets of
every size, the remainder falls with the fine-tuning scale at close to the
predicted exponent, and three-way donor assignments are predicted as well as
single swaps (Figure~\ref{fig:verify} and Table~\ref{tab:verify-full}). The
harder question is whether the score needs everything in it.
The appendix records a frozen four-donor follow-up and signed-knapsack
tightness check; these are controlled-stack diagnostics, not language-model
multi-donor evidence.
Table~\ref{tab:verify} removes one factor at a time from
$\langle s_\ell, v_\ell\rangle$. The comparison is set up so that it does not
favour the proposed score. Windows have fixed width, which prevents a scorer summing
non-negative magnitudes from winning by simply taking the largest window, and is
also the design the layer-swap literature actually sweeps. The per-layer expert
gaps span a $4\times$ range, which gives the expert-intrinsic criterion real
variation to rank; identical perturbation scales would make
$\|\Delta\theta_\ell\|$ nearly constant and uninformative.

The right column to read is regret, which unlike a rank correlation does not
depend on a sign an unsigned baseline could not have known in advance.
\textsc{Suture} selects at regret $0.03$. Dropping the adjoint gives $0.26$,
dropping the injection $0.25$, and parameter-change magnitude $0.29$, against
$0.33$ for random scores. Neither factor survives alone; the product carries the
signal. The last row is a control: scoring for one readout and ranking against a
competing one gives
$-0.29\pm0.52$, so the score is specific to the behaviour it was built for.

\paragraph{What the larger design space buys, and where it stops.}
The unrestricted optimiser costs the same to score and beats the best contiguous
window at every tolerance tested, by at least $82\%$ of that window's own effect,
from $24$ numbers enumeration would have replaced with $4096$ models
(Table~\ref{tab:design}); whether that transfers to language models is untested.
Figure~\ref{fig:verify}(c) is the limitation the method must own: rank correlation
is $1.00$ at small and moderate scales while relative magnitude error is already
$9$--$29\%$, and past a scale of roughly $0.4$ it falls to $0.73$. \textsc{Suture}
ranks; it does not predict how much a composition will help.

\section{Language-model measurements}
\label{sec:protocol}
% ============================================================================

Synthetic checks cannot establish whether teacher-forced rankings survive to
free generation. The local evaluations stopped at their pre-specified gates;
B3 is a bounded real-model candidate comparison, not a free-generation ranking
result.

\paragraph{Qwen2.5-Coder: the ranking run has limited power.}
\texttt{Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct}, matched LoRA adapters (128 steps,
$10^{-5}$), was the only cached 1--2B decoder available offline; its
code-specialised pretraining also limits interpretation. Donor exact match
$0.03125\to 0.0625$ in Spanish and Chinese, zero gain in Swahili. Probes
$|\mathcal{P}_\util|=|\mathcal{P}_\risk|=200$, $|\mathcal{C}|=500$, $n=8$ MGSM-dev items
\citep{shi2022mgsm}, five decode seeds. Canonical E1 did not read MGSM-test.
Teacher-forced log-probability on those eight items ranks at Spearman $0.877$;
free-generation exact match does not ($-0.018$). A later isolated $64$-item
greedy ranking on the same scores reaches Spearman $0.129$ and still misses the
$0.5$ gate (Appendix~\ref{app:notes}).
The fixed seven-score, $24$-window comparison in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}
provides no baseline-based rescue.

\paragraph{What that $-0.018$ can and cannot carry.}
The eight-item design is forty Bernoulli trials per window at a rate near $0.1$.
A null predictor already spans Spearman $[-0.101,+0.097]$ at 95\%, so $-0.018$ is
chance ($z=-0.36$); a perfect ranker reaches $0.5$ only if the true window spread
exceeds about $0.10$, wider than this sweep (Table~\ref{tab:power}). The selected
window versus the sweep optimum is $3/40$ against $5/40$ ($p=0.73$).

\paragraph{B3: a real 8B scale check.}
On an A100-SXM4-80GB, frozen B3 selection scored all $666$ intervals with zero
grafted-model builds. On disjoint $250$-record test manifests, selected
intervals $[13,15]$ (French) and $[20,31]$ (Chinese) gained $+0.398$ and
$+1.326$ nats/token over the host (paired bootstrap $95\%$ intervals
$[+0.358,+0.437]$ and $[+1.210,+1.446]$; Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). This
three-candidate teacher-forced comparison is not a full-window or
free-generation ranking.

\paragraph{Qwen3-1.7B: the attempt stops earlier.}
On \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B} \citep{yang2025qwen3} Spanish passed host LID
($0.914$) then failed a synthetic-donor check (Spearman $0.673$ vs $0.90$). A
frozen repair makes the zero-donor invariant exact, but fake-donor checks stay
below threshold; the real donor reaches $0.917$ on $17$ same-probe grafts and
does not authorize E1. Chinese and Swahili failed host LID at $0.891$ and
$0.789$. Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} has the pinned rows.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{Layer swapping, and what merging criteria look at.}
\citet{bandarkar2025layerswap} introduced layer swapping, reading the band from
parameter movement; later work applies it to long-CoT reasoning
\citep{bandarkar2025unreasonable, lasbordes2026rethinking} and safety
\citep{shin2026safetyswap}. The lineage sits inside task arithmetic
\citep{ilharco2023task, chronopoulou2024langtask} and composable sparse
fine-tuning \citep{ansell2022sft}. Expert-intrinsic criteria use parameter
movement, task-vector interference, localisation, activations, saliency, or
curvature \citep{yadav2023ties, yu2024dare, he2024localize, acm2025,
jin2023regmean, park2026samerging, xia2026fimmerging}; behaviour-referenced
criteria optimise losses on data \citep{yang2024adamerging, supermerge2024,
expertmerging2025, lotmerging2025, symerge2026, outputspace2026, fu2026merit}.
Layer-wise Bayesian search uses a discrepancy prior but still queries candidate
merges \citep{chen2025sipbmm}. Dynamic Weight Grafting instead localises
fine-tuned factual knowledge by dynamically combining weights during inference
\citep{nief2026grafting}; it is complementary to, not a selector for, a fixed
host--donor graft. Existing approaches either read experts alone or materialise
candidates; none predicts a behaviour-specific additive score.

\paragraph{Low-cost proxies, and what they cannot say.}
\citet{tap2026} use an unlabelled task-alignment proxy, while
\citet{merge3-2025, zhou2026mergeability, simmerge2026} estimate merge quality
from item responses or observed merges. We claim no novelty in using a proxy.
Existing proxies score one configuration at a time and are commonly validated by
aggregate rank correlation, which can fail on top-ranked candidates; our identity
decomposes over units and supports a per-instance certificate.

\paragraph{Linearity and first-order attribution.}
Near-linearity in task vectors \citep{ortizjimenez2023tangent, dai2025submodule}
and first-order activation attribution
\citep{nanda2023attribution, syed2024attribution, kramar2024atp,
hanna2024eapig, olivieri2026tft, jantsch2026dpa} are established. Layer
Gradient Analysis uses gradients to identify useful editing layers
\citep{datta2026lga}. DPA and LGA are attribution or editing-localisation
methods, not drop-in baselines for discrete cross-model graft selection; those
works operate within one model, whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition} treats
discrete parameter grafts across models. Within-model layer replacement is complementary
\citep{garcia2026nofreeswap}; Appendix~\ref{app:positioning} discusses where
language information resides in the stack.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}}
\label{app:proof-superposition}
% ============================================================================

Write $a(\ell)=\donor$ if $\ell\in S$ and $\host$ otherwise, and recall
$\Delta_\ell = x^{S}_\ell - x^{\host}_\ell$ with $\Delta_0=0$.

\paragraph{Assumption~\ref{ass:smooth}, in full.}
Fix $R>0$ and let $\mathcal{B}_R$ be the union of the balls of radius $R$ about
the host states $x^{\host}_\ell$. On $\mathcal{B}_R$ each unit map
$x\mapsto B_\ell(x;\theta)$ is $\Lambda$-Lipschitz with $\kappa$-Lipschitz
Jacobian, for $\theta$ ranging over the host and every donor parameter in play,
and $\|J^{(j)}_\ell - J^{\host}_\ell\|\le\lambda$ for every donor $j$, where
$J^{\bullet}_\ell$ is the Jacobian of unit $\ell$ at $x^{\host}_\ell$. We further
require $A\varepsilon_S\le R$ with $A=(1+\Lambda)^{L}$, which keeps every graft
trajectory inside $\mathcal{B}_R$; since $A$ depends on $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda$ on
$R$, the two must be chosen together rather than in sequence. With these
constants the remainder of Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition} satisfies the explicit
bound $\|R(S)\| \le \Gamma L A\,\varepsilon_S(\lambda + \tfrac{\kappa}{2}A\varepsilon_S)$,
where $\Gamma = \max_\ell\|P_{\ell+1:L}\|$.

\begin{corollary}[Continuous coefficients]
\label{cor:alpha}
Interpolating a unit towards its donor,
$\theta_\ell(\alpha_\ell)=\theta^{\host}_\ell+\alpha_\ell(\theta^{\donor}_\ell-\theta^{\host}_\ell)$,
gives a first-order readout change $\sum_\ell \alpha_\ell a^{\varphi}_\ell$ plus
the Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition} remainder and an extra scalar $\rho$. That
term is the error of linearising the block in its \emph{parameters}, which the
discrete case never incurs because it evaluates the donor block exactly, and it
is not covered by Assumption~\ref{ass:smooth}. If $B_\ell$ is twice
differentiable in $\theta$ with $\|\partial^2_\theta B_\ell\|\le\kappa_\theta$ on
the segment, then
$|\rho| \le \|\nabla\varphi(x^{\host}_L)\|\,\Gamma\sum_\ell \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha_\ell(1-\alpha_\ell)\,\kappa_\theta\|\theta^{\donor}_\ell-\theta^{\host}_\ell\|^2$
up to the usual first-order remainder in $\Delta_L$. The constant is the
\emph{size} of the gradient at the host endpoint, not the Lipschitz constant
$L_\varphi$ of the gradient: $\rho$ arises from contracting propagated injection
deviations with $\nabla\varphi$, and a linear readout has $L_\varphi=0$ while
$\rho\neq 0$. The bound vanishes at $\alpha_\ell\in\{0,1\}$, as it must, since
both endpoints are evaluated exactly. Nothing in the main text uses
$\alpha\notin\{0,1\}$.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Step 1: an exact recursion.}
By definition of the two trajectories,
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\ell+1}
&= \Delta_\ell + B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell+\Delta_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell\right) - B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\host}_\ell\right) \nonumber\\
&= \Delta_\ell
+ \underbrace{\left[B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell\right) - B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\host}_\ell\right)\right]}_{=\,\mathbf{1}[\ell\in S]\, v_\ell}
+ \underbrace{\left[B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell+\Delta_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell\right) - B_\ell\!\left(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell\right)\right]}_{\text{propagation}} .
\label{eq:exact-recursion}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:exact-recursion} is exact, with no approximation; the split
adds and subtracts $B_\ell(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell)$. It is verified
numerically to relative error $1.9\times10^{-15}$ in
Table~\ref{tab:verify-full}.

\paragraph{Step 2: separate the host propagator.}
A first-order Taylor expansion of the propagation term about $x^{\host}_\ell$
with $\kappa$-Lipschitz Jacobian gives
$B_\ell(x^{\host}_\ell+\Delta_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell) - B_\ell(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{a(\ell)}_\ell) = J^{a(\ell)}_\ell \Delta_\ell + r_\ell$
with $\|r_\ell\| \le \tfrac{\kappa}{2}\|\Delta_\ell\|^2$. Using
$J^{a(\ell)}_\ell = J^{\host}_\ell + \mathbf{1}[\ell\in S](J^{\donor}_\ell - J^{\host}_\ell)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:host-recursion}
\Delta_{\ell+1} = \left(I + J^{\host}_\ell\right)\Delta_\ell + \mathbf{1}[\ell\in S]\, v_\ell + q_\ell,
\qquad
\|q_\ell\| \le \lambda\|\Delta_\ell\| + \tfrac{\kappa}{2}\|\Delta_\ell\|^2 .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 3: an a priori bound on the deviation.}
Independently of Step 2, Lipschitz continuity of the active block applied to
\eqref{eq:exact-recursion} gives
$\|\Delta_{\ell+1}\| \le (1+\Lambda)\|\Delta_\ell\| + \mathbf{1}[\ell\in S]\|v_\ell\|$,
so by induction, with $A=(1+\Lambda)^L$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:apriori}
\|\Delta_\ell\| \;\le\; \sum_{k\in S,\, k<\ell} (1+\Lambda)^{\ell-1-k}\|v_k\| \;\le\; A\,\varepsilon_S .
\end{equation}
The induction is valid exactly while each $x^{\host}_\ell+\Delta_\ell$ remains in
$\mathcal{B}_R$, which is the condition $A\varepsilon_S\le R$ of
Assumption~\ref{ass:smooth}; no smallness beyond that is used.

\paragraph{Step 4: unroll and collect.}
Unrolling \eqref{eq:host-recursion} from $\ell=0$ to $L$,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_L = \sum_{\ell\in S} P_{\ell+1:L}\, v_\ell + \sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1} P_{\ell+1:L}\, q_\ell ,
\end{equation}
which identifies $R(S) = \sum_\ell P_{\ell+1:L} q_\ell$. Substituting
\eqref{eq:apriori} into the bound on $\|q_\ell\|$ from \eqref{eq:host-recursion}
and using $\|P_{\ell+1:L}\|\le\Gamma$,
\begin{equation}
\|R(S)\| \;\le\; \Gamma\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}\left(\lambda A\varepsilon_S + \tfrac{\kappa}{2}A^2\varepsilon_S^2\right)
= \Gamma L A \varepsilon_S\left(\lambda + \tfrac{\kappa}{2}A\varepsilon_S\right),
\end{equation}
which is the explicit form of the $O(\varepsilon_S(\lambda+\varepsilon_S))$
remainder in \eqref{eq:superposition}. If each unit $\ell$ is assigned a donor
$j(\ell)\in\{1,\dots,k\}$ rather than a single donor, the same unrolling holds
with $v_\ell$ the injection of the assigned donor and
$\lambda=\max_j\lambda_j$. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Corollary~\ref{cor:scoring}.}
For $\varphi$ with $L_\varphi$-Lipschitz gradient,
$\varphi(x^{S}_L)-\varphi(x^{\host}_L) = \langle\nabla\varphi(x^{\host}_L),\Delta_L\rangle + \rho$
with $|\rho|\le \tfrac{L_\varphi}{2}\|\Delta_L\|^2$. Substituting
\eqref{eq:superposition} and using
$\langle\nabla\varphi(x^{\host}_L), P_{\ell+1:L} v_\ell\rangle = \langle P_{\ell+1:L}^{\top}\nabla\varphi(x^{\host}_L), v_\ell\rangle = \langle s^{\varphi}_\ell, v_\ell\rangle$
gives the claim; that $s^{\varphi}_\ell = \partial\varphi/\partial x_{\ell+1}$ on
the host trajectory, and that all $L$ of them come from one reverse-mode sweep,
is the definition of reverse-mode differentiation applied to the host stack.
The injections themselves are off-path donor evaluations at those host states.
\hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha}.}
Write $\Delta\theta_\ell=\theta^{\donor}_\ell-\theta^{\host}_\ell$ and
$v_\ell(\alpha)=B_\ell(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\host}_\ell+\alpha\Delta\theta_\ell)-B_\ell(x^{\host}_\ell;\theta^{\host}_\ell)$,
so that $v_\ell(1)=v_\ell$ is the discrete injection. Write
$r_\ell(\alpha)=v_\ell(\alpha)-\alpha v_\ell(1)$. Then $r_\ell(0)=r_\ell(1)=0$.
If $B_\ell$ is $C^2$ in $\theta$ on the segment, Taylor expansion with integral
remainder gives
$\|r_\ell(\alpha)\|\le\tfrac12\alpha(1-\alpha)\kappa_\theta\|\Delta\theta_\ell\|^2$,
without treating $\partial^2_\theta B_\ell$ as a vector-valued mean-value
identity. Propagating these injection deviations with the host propagator, as in
Step 4 of Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}, and contracting with
$\nabla\varphi(x^{\host}_L)$ produces a \emph{scalar} remainder $\rho$ bounded by
$\|\nabla\varphi(x^{\host}_L)\|\,\Gamma\sum_\ell \tfrac12\alpha_\ell(1-\alpha_\ell)\kappa_\theta\|\Delta\theta_\ell\|^2$
up to the usual first-order remainder in $\Delta_L$. The factor $\alpha(1-\alpha)$ is not cosmetic: at
$\alpha\in\{0,1\}$ the unit is evaluated exactly, so the deviation must be zero,
and the bound respects that. Verified in the released harness: the deviation
follows $\alpha(1-\alpha)$ to within $0.1\%$ across five values of $\alpha$, is
zero at both endpoints to machine precision, and scales as $\|\Delta\theta\|^{2}$
with a fitted log-log exponent of $1.996$. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:regret}.}
Since $\widehat{S}$ maximises $A$ over $\mathcal{F}$, we have
$A(\widehat{S})\ge A(S^{\circ})$ for $S^{\circ}\in\mathcal{F}$, so
\[
\Delta\varphi(S^{\circ})-\Delta\varphi(\widehat{S})
= \big(A(S^{\circ})-A(\widehat{S})\big) + r(S^{\circ}) - r(\widehat{S})
\;\le\; r(S^{\circ}) - r(\widehat{S}) \;\le\; 2\sup_{S\in\mathcal{F}}|r(S)| .
\]
For the margin condition, suppose $A(\widehat{S})-A(S)>|r(S)|+|r(\widehat{S})|$
for all $S\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{\widehat{S}\}$. Then
$\Delta\varphi(\widehat S)-\Delta\varphi(S) = A(\widehat S)-A(S) + r(\widehat S)-r(S) > 0$,
so $\widehat S$ is the strict maximiser. For the last clause, let
$S_1,\dots,S_m,S_{m+1}$ be drawn uniformly without replacement from
$\mathcal{F}\setminus\{\widehat S\}$.
The values $|r(S_1)|,\dots,|r(S_{m+1})|$ are exchangeable, so
$|r(S_{m+1})|$ is equally likely to occupy any rank among the $m+1$; it exceeds
all of the first $m$ with probability at most $1/(m+1)$, with equality when ties
have probability zero. \hfill$\square$

Two things this does not say. It does not bound the regret against an adversarial
competitor: the last clause is a statement about a uniformly drawn alternative
from $\mathcal{F}$, not a supremum over the comparison set, and a competitor
chosen to have a large
remainder is not covered. And it inherits the caveat of
the explicit constant above: the deterministic form is only as useful as the
available control on $\sup_S|r(S)|$, which is why the computable form is the one
we use. Verified in the released harness: the realised regret respects the bound,
the oracle margin condition certifies $75$ of $77$ competitors on this seeded
stack with no false certification, and the deployable form certifies
$64.8\pm9.6$ of $77$ competitors across $12$ stacks from $m{=}10$ built
competitors. The exceedance rate of the conformal quantile tracks
$1/(m+1)$ at $m\in\{10,20,40\}$, at $0.098$, $0.044$ and $0.023$ against nominal
$0.091$, $0.048$ and $0.024$. The $m=10$ estimate sits $1.4$ Monte Carlo standard
errors above its nominal value over $3000$ draws, which we report rather than
round away.

\paragraph{What the theorem does not say.}
It does not say the remainder is small at any particular scale, and
the constant is exponential in depth and cannot be used numerically.
It does not license using $\sum_{\ell\in S} a^{\varphi}_\ell$ as an estimate of
how much a graft will help; Figure~\ref{fig:verify}(c) shows magnitudes carry
$9$--$29\%$ relative error even where ranking is exact. It assumes the same
architecture and a shared base, and it assumes differentiability, which excludes
hard-routed mixture-of-experts blocks without smoothing.

\section{Standard machinery: optimisers and the drift bound}
\label{app:proof-optimiser}
% ============================================================================

Nothing in this appendix is new. It is collected here so the main text carries
only the results that are, and so that the claims can be checked.

\paragraph{(a) Contiguous windows in $O(L\log L)$.}
The empty graft is always feasible and has value $0$, so it is kept as a
candidate and returned whenever no window beats it; without this the solver is
forced to graft something even when every feasible window is worse than the host.
Let $U_0=R_0=0$ and $U_b=\sum_{\ell<b}a^{\util}_\ell$, $R_b=\sum_{\ell<b}a^{\risk}_\ell$.
The window $[i,j]$ has value $U_{j+1}-U_i$ and risk $R_{j+1}-R_i$, so the
program is
\begin{equation}
\max_{0\le i < b \le L}\; U_b - U_i
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad R_i \le R_b + \tau .
\end{equation}
Sweeping $b$ upward and inserting $(R_i,U_i)$ for $i=b-1$ before querying, the
inner problem is $\min\{U_i : R_i \le R_b+\tau\}$, a prefix-minimum query over a
key that is known offline. Coordinate-compressing the $L+1$ values of $R$ and
using a Fenwick tree of prefix minima gives $O(\log L)$ per insert and per
query, hence $O(L\log L)$ overall. Correctness is that every feasible window is
enumerated exactly once as a pair $(i,b)$. Agreement with the $O(L^2)$ brute
force on $400$ random instances is reported in Table~\ref{tab:verify-full}.

\paragraph{(b) Arbitrary subsets.}
With both signs present the program is a $0/1$ knapsack with signed weights,
which is NP-hard in general, so we solve it pseudo-polynomially. Let
$\mathrm{lo}=\sum_{\ell: a^{\risk}_\ell<0}a^{\risk}_\ell$,
$\mathrm{hi}=\sum_{\ell: a^{\risk}_\ell>0}a^{\risk}_\ell$ and put a uniform grid
of $N$ cells of width $h=(\mathrm{hi}-\mathrm{lo})/N$ on $[\mathrm{lo},\mathrm{hi}]$
(if $\mathrm{hi}=\mathrm{lo}$ every risk score is zero, the constraint is vacuous
and the unconstrained maximiser is returned).
Round each weight conservatively: a negative $a^{\risk}_\ell$ is charged
$-h\lceil -a^{\risk}_\ell/h\rceil$, which is at least as negative as the truth,
and a positive one is credited $h\lfloor a^{\risk}_\ell/h\rfloor$, which is at
most the truth. Any set feasible on the grid then satisfies the true constraint,
so the DP never returns a violating solution. Because each of the at most $L$
selected items is mis-stated by less than $h$, the grid feasible set contains
every set with true slack at least $Lh$, so the optimality gap is at most the
best objective attainable in that slack margin and is one-sided. The DP itself
is the standard $O(LN)$ table. On $60$ random instances at $L=12$ checked
against exhaustive $2^{12}$ search there were $0$ violations at every grid
resolution, and the maximum gap fell from $1.8\times10^{-1}$ at $N=200$ to
$0$ at $N=5000$ (Table~\ref{tab:verify-full}). An expanded follow-up used
$80$ fresh instances at each of $N=100,500,2000,10000,50000$: violations
remained $0$, while the maximum observed objective gaps were
$0.9862,0.7051,0,0,0$, respectively. These are empirical tightness
diagnostics, not a worst-case optimality guarantee. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Drift certificate (optional post-selection procedure).}
$\widehat{S}$ is a function of the probe set only. If the calibration prompts
are drawn independently from the deployment distribution, paired with independent
decode seeds, and are independent of that probe set, then conditional on
$\widehat{S}$ the indicators
$Z_1,\dots,Z_n$ are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli$(\pi)$ and
$\sum_i Z_i \sim \mathrm{Bin}(n, \pi)$ with $\pi$ the population drift rate.
Exchangeability of the $Z_i$ is not enough for this likelihood. The
Clopper--Pearson upper limit $\bar{u}_\delta(k,n)$ is by construction the
largest $\pi$ with $\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n,\pi)\le k]>\delta$, hence
$\Pr[\pi\le\bar{u}_\delta(\sum_i Z_i,n)]\ge 1-\delta$. Certifying $K$
candidates at level $\delta/K$ and taking a union bound gives the simultaneous
statement. Simulated coverage at nominal $0.95$ is $0.971$--$1.000$ across the
drift rates tested (Table~\ref{tab:verify-full}); the conservatism is the known
discreteness of the exact binomial interval. Reusing one prompt with several
decode seeds, as in the reported $500\times5$ audit, does not establish this
i.i.d.\ premise; its bound is consequently reported as conditional rather than
as a prompt-level guarantee. \hfill$\square$

\section{Limitations in full}
\label{app:limits}
% ============================================================================

The linearisation has a regime, mapped in Figure~\ref{fig:verify}(c) and
measured for the three real expert pairs in the regime diagnostic. The Spanish ranking result
cannot place that pair on either side of the regime boundary. The readouts
include teacher-forced log-probabilities, while the failure that matters is a
property of free generation; the ranking protocol measures that gap
directly.
Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition}'s constant is exponential in depth and is not
usable numerically. The optional certificate bounds
drift under i.i.d.\ calibration draws, says nothing about task accuracy, and
fails under shift. Both experts must share an architecture
and a base checkpoint, as in all layer-swap work, and the utility probe relies on
machine translation; a translation ablation was specified but was not run. Two
negative findings are recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:negative}: a conjecture that
the realised error tracks the donor--host Jacobian gap at fixed weight distance
was not supported, so no claim rests on it; and a
trained-from-scratch transformer testbed in which the transfer phenomenon did not
emerge at all, which constrains the proposed language-model evaluation to
genuinely pretrained multilingual bases. A third diagnostic on the Spanish sweep:
if every window shared a true rate of $0.1$, the
maximum over a sweep this size would be near $0.26$, above the $0.125$ observed,
so most windows sit near zero and the ground truth is dominated by ties. With ties
or noise, a rank correlation against it carries no signal.

Finally, the Spanish seed-0 language-model ranking is not a negative result about
the scores. Its ground truth is $40$ Bernoulli trials per window, under which an
uninformative predictor already spans $[-0.101,+0.097]$ and a perfect ranker
tops out near the $0.5$ threshold (Table~\ref{tab:power}); the observed $-0.018$ lies
inside the null band. The run is uninformative, and we report it as such rather
than as a failure of the method or as a success of it. A later $64$-item greedy
test ranking on the same scores reaches Spearman $0.129$, above a chance band
near $[-0.10,+0.10]$, but selected exact match is $1/64$ against a sweep best of
$2/64$ and the $0.5$ gate is still missed. Chinese and Swahili
free-generation ranking was not run. B3 is stronger real-model evidence, but its
held-out stage is a three-candidate teacher-forced comparison, not a ranking
sweep; no free-generation claim follows.

% ============================================================================
\section{Negative results and excluded claims}
\label{app:negative}
% ============================================================================

\paragraph{An unsupported conjecture: the $\lambda$ factor is not predictive.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition} bounds the remainder by a product containing the
donor--host Jacobian gap $\lambda$. A stronger, testable interpretation would
separate the analysis from a generic Taylor argument: with
the weight perturbation norm held fixed, expert pairs whose Jacobians agree more
closely should show a smaller linearisation error. We tested this directly in the
controlled stack, sampling fourteen donors at a weight distance held constant to
within $0.00\%$ and varying only the direction. The correlation between $\lambda$
and the realised error was $-0.304$, the wrong sign, while weight distance, held
fixed by construction, correlated at $0.000$. The conjecture is not supported;
no claim in this paper rests on it. Theorem~\ref{thm:superposition} is unaffected,
since an upper bound containing $\lambda$ stays valid whether or not $\lambda$ is
tight; what fails is the stronger reading of $\lambda$ as a predictor. The check
is retained in the released harness as a negative result.

\paragraph{A testbed that did not reproduce the phenomenon.}
Section~\ref{sec:verify} verifies the mathematics on a stack whose experts are
constructed rather than trained, so it does not test transfer learned from
pretraining. We
therefore built a small transformer with attention, layer normalisation and GELU
MLPs, wrote its backward pass by hand and gradient-checked it to a worst relative
error of $9\times10^{-9}$, pretrained it on a task whose input script and output
language are decoupled so that a script-independent value representation is
forced, and fine-tuned a skill expert and a language expert from that shared
base. The target cell, the skill in the language nobody was trained on, was left
genuinely unseen. Across two designs of the pretraining task no graft improved on
the host, and the best contiguous window was within noise of the host itself. One
hypothesis is that the phenomenon needs a base
whose multilingual representations are strong enough to survive expert
fine-tuning, which a small model trained from scratch on a synthetic task does not
have. The practical consequence is that the proposed language-model evaluation
should use a genuinely pretrained multilingual base; substituting a small
from-scratch model would not isolate the intended setting from the selection
method. The testbed is released so that the negative result is reproducible.

\paragraph{The two readout functionals, written out.}
Let $q$ be a probe question and $y$ a continuation. $\varphi_\util(p)$ is the
length-normalised teacher-forced log-probability of the reference solution $y^{\star}$
given the machine-translated question, $\tfrac{1}{|y^{\star}|}\log p(y^{\star}\mid q)$,
with $y^{\star}$ inherited from the English source so no annotator is involved.
$\varphi_\risk(p)$ is the length-normalised margin
$\tfrac{1}{|y^{\mathrm{t}}|}\log p(y^{\mathrm{t}}\mid c)-\tfrac{1}{|y^{\mathrm{d}}|}\log p(y^{\mathrm{d}}\mid c)$
between a target-language continuation $y^{\mathrm{t}}$ and its donor-language
translation $y^{\mathrm{d}}$ under the shared prefix $c$, so importing the donor
lowers it and the constraint $\Delta\varphi_\risk(S)\ge-\tau$ reads as a bound on
how much host-language preference may be given up. Length normalisation matters:
without it both functionals are dominated by continuation length rather than by
the graft.

\section{Where language lives in the stack}
\label{app:positioning}

That early layers align input, middle layers compute in a largely
language-agnostic space, and late layers commit to the output language is
supported by logit-lens \citep{wendler2024llamas}, neuron-level
\citep{tang2024langneurons} and behavioural \citep{etxaniz2024think,
schut2025english, zhao2024multilingualism} evidence. It is why a graft band
exists; it does not say where that band ends for a given model and language.
Adjacent steering work also picks intervention depths by sweep and names the cost
explicitly \citep{multilingualsteering2026, linguamap2026}.

\section{Notes for the experimental tier}

\paragraph{Streaming the donor.}
Line 4 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:suture} needs the donor's unit $\ell$ only while
unit $\ell$ is being scored, so peak memory is the host plus one unit rather
than two full models. On a shared base, storing the donor as a task vector
further reduces the footprint.

\paragraph{Cost accounting.}
Let $|\mathcal{P}_\varphi|$ be the probe count for readout $\varphi$, $C_f$ the
cost of a host forward pass, $C_d$ the cost of streaming all donor-block
evaluations for one probe, and $C_b$ a backward pass. Selection costs
$\sum_{\varphi}|\mathcal{P}_\varphi|(C_f+C_d+C_b)$ and zero model builds. An
exhaustive contiguous sweep costs
$\tfrac{L(L+1)}{2}\left(C_{\mathrm{build}} + C_{\mathrm{bench}}\right)$ where
$C_{\mathrm{bench}}$ is a full generative benchmark run. The ratio is reported
as measured wall-clock where a complete uninterrupted run exists. After resume
the Spanish selection time is unknown and the recorded sweep time covers only
the remaining windows. The accounting identity is that selection cost does not
grow with the number of candidate windows. Table~\ref{tab:cost-followup}
measures the individual stages on two local pairs; its directional
Jacobian quantities are local probes, not estimates of the theorem's
worst-case constants.
On the Qwen3-1.7B row of that table, scoring two prompts costs $3.854$\,s,
while $406$ two-layer candidate forwards cost $161$\,s ($42\times$) and a
full-forward lower bound for the same $406$ candidates is $806$\,s
($209\times$). Streamed donor evaluation peaks $0.81$\,GiB above the host
forward. These are local stage timings, not 8B FLOPs.

\paragraph{Baselines already in the ablation.}
Dropping the injection and ranking with the adjoint alone is the natural
within-model gradient-layer selector in this setting. Table~\ref{tab:verify}
records that this raises selection regret from $0.03$ to $0.26$, near random.
Proposition~\ref{prop:regret}'s remainder $r(S)$ is always measured on the
same teacher-forced readout used for selection; substituting free-generation
exact match would be a different claim, and the Spanish Coder run already
shows that the two orderings need not agree.

\paragraph{Probe-size smoke.}
An eight-probe scoring run on the same Coder pair yields window Spearman
$0.93$ against the $200$-probe scores, while the unconstrained utility
argmax moves from $[1,27]$ to $[0,27]$. Per-layer Spearman is only $0.59$.
We treat $200$ probes as the operating size and do not claim a minimum.

\paragraph{Powered Spanish ranking follow-up.}
A separate contract reused the frozen Coder scores and decoded all $406$
windows greedily on $64$ hash-frozen MGSM-test items
(\texttt{results/reviewer\_followup/powered\_spanish\_ranking/}).
Free-generation Spearman is $0.129$ (above a $64$-trial null band near
$[-0.10,+0.10]$), the selected window $[1,27]$ scores $1/64$, and the
sweep best is $2/64$. Exact match sits on $\{0,1,2\}$ successes, so the
pre-specified $0.5$ rank gate remains out of reach. This is a more
powered negative on the same pair, not a free-generation ranking success.
Canonical E1 was not rewritten.

\paragraph{Off-path evaluation.}
Donor blocks are evaluated at the host residual state with eager attention,
BF16, and no gradient through the donor. Rotary embeddings and RMSNorm
follow the host cache at that layer; dropout is off at eval. Cached and
uncached greedy paths are required to match when generation is scored; a
BF16 Qwen3-8B check that they do not is why that free-generation panel was
not reported.
